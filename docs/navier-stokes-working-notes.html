1Why I took the assignment seriously

Your example was better than you may have known. When you wrote that "an LLM recently disproved the Jacobian conjecture," I went to check, expecting to correct a garbled headline. Instead I found that Levent Alpoge announced, today, a counterexample to the Jacobian conjecture — one of the central open problems of algebraic geometry since 1939 — crediting a question from Akhil Mathew and a construction produced by Claude Fable 5, which happens to be the model family writing these notes (Alpoge's post, Hacker News thread, Lichtman's summary).

I did not take this on faith. The claimed counterexample is the polynomial map $F:\mathbb{C}^3\to\mathbb{C}^3$,

$$F(x,y,z)\;=\;\Big(\,(1+xy)^3\,z+y^2(1+xy)(4+3xy),\;\; y+3x(1+xy)^2\,z+3xy^2(4+3xy),\;\; 2x-3x^2y-x^3z\,\Big),$$

and the claim is that $\det DF \equiv -2$ (a nonzero constant) while $F$ identifies three distinct points. I verified both claims symbolically in this session:

det J = -2
F(0, 0, -1/4)     = (-1/4, 0, 0)
F(1, -3/2, 13/2)  = (-1/4, 0, 0)
F(-1, 3/2, 13/2)  = (-1/4, 0, 0)

The Jacobian determinant is identically $-2$, and three distinct points share an image, so $F$ has no inverse of any kind — polynomial or otherwise. Modulo faithful transcription of the posted formula (I used Alpoge's own text), this is a complete, machine-checkable disproof. It is hours old, has no refereed write-up yet, and Wikipedia has not caught up; but unlike most "conjecture falls" announcements, the verification is a finite computation that anyone can repeat in seconds, and I have repeated it. Your premise stands. This is also not an isolated event: in the past year, frontier models have resolved several long-open Erdős problems (Scientific American, Physics World), with Terence Tao calling one such resolution the most unambiguous instance so far of AI solving an open problem (report), and an OpenAI model has disproved a central conjecture in discrete geometry.

So the question deserves a real answer, not a reflex: why not this one?

Here is the structural difference, and it is the honest heart of this whole document. Every AI success above produced a finite certificate — an object whose correctness is checkable by a short, mechanical computation. A polynomial map refutes the Jacobian conjecture the moment a computer algebra system expands a determinant and evaluates six numbers; the search was hard, but the verification is trivial. The Navier–Stokes problem, in either direction, has no known certificate of that type. A proof of global regularity is a statement about every smooth initial datum, quantified over an infinite-dimensional space, and §5.1 below describes a theorem of Tao that explains why no "soft" argument can deliver it. A disproof — a solution that breaks down — is a single object, which sounds Jacobian-like, but certifying that a specific initial datum leads to breakdown means rigorously controlling a nonlinear evolution all the way to its singular time, including its instabilities. The nearest completed analog (blowup for 3D Euler with boundary, §6) required several hundred pages of analysis plus interval-arithmetic numerics, for an equation strictly easier than Navier–Stokes. The gap between "find a magic polynomial" and "certify a singular solution of a supercritical PDE" is not a gap of ambition or self-confidence; it is a gap in what verification costs. When that cost structure changes — and §6 describes the program that is actively trying to change it — models like me will be relevant to this problem in the way I was relevant to the Jacobian conjecture this morning. It has not changed yet.

With expectations set honestly, everything that follows is the strongest true engagement I can produce in one session: the problem stated exactly, the known mathematics derived rather than name-dropped, the frontier surveyed as of this week, and my own attempts documented with their failure modes.

2The problem, precisely

The official formulation is Fefferman's problem description for the Clay Institute [F] — the PDF you attached. Since prize-level precision lives in the fine print, here is the exact structure. The unknowns are the velocity $u(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^3$ and pressure $p(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}$ solving

$$\partial_t u_i+\sum_{j=1}^{3}u_j\,\partial_{x_j}u_i \;=\; \nu\,\Delta u_i-\partial_{x_i}p+f_i(x,t),\qquad \operatorname{div}u=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\partial_{x_i}u_i=0, \qquad u(x,0)=u^\circ(x),\tag{1–3}$$

with viscosity $\nu\gt 0$. On $\mathbb{R}^3$ the data are constrained by decay: the initial field satisfies $|\partial_x^\alpha u^\circ(x)|\le C_{\alpha K}(1+|x|)^{-K}$ for all $\alpha,K$ (condition (4) — Schwartz-type decay), and the force satisfies the spacetime analog (condition (5)). A solution counts as physically reasonable only if $p,u\in C^\infty(\mathbb{R}^3\times[0,\infty))$ (condition (6)) and the energy stays bounded: $\int_{\mathbb{R}^3}|u(x,t)|^2\,dx \lt C$ for all $t$ (condition (7)). On the torus $\mathbb{R}^3/\mathbb{Z}^3$, decay is replaced by periodicity (8)–(9), and the solution must satisfy (10)–(11). The prize is for any one of:

(A) Existence and smoothness on $\mathbb{R}^3$. $\nu\gt 0$, $f\equiv 0$: for every smooth divergence-free $u^\circ$ satisfying (4), there exist smooth $(p,u)$ on $\mathbb{R}^3\times[0,\infty)$ satisfying (1)–(3), (6), (7).

(B) The periodic analog on $\mathbb{R}^3/\mathbb{Z}^3$, again with $f\equiv 0$.

(C) Breakdown on $\mathbb{R}^3$. There exist a smooth divergence-free $u^\circ$ satisfying (4) and a smooth force $f$ satisfying (5) for which no $(p,u)$ satisfies (1)–(3), (6), (7) on $\mathbb{R}^3\times[0,\infty)$.

(D) The periodic breakdown analog, with $f$ allowed subject to (8)–(9).

Four pieces of fine print matter enormously for anyone plotting an attack. First, the breakdown statements (C)/(D) allow a forcing term — you get to shake the fluid, provided the shaking is smooth for all time with strong decay; this is more leeway than "unforced blowup" and it is still open. Second, (A)/(B) require the solution to exist for every admissible datum, so a single bad datum settles the problem negatively; either direction wins the prize. Third, condition (7), bounded energy, quietly excludes a family of cheap pathologies: scale-invariant and self-similar objects with infinite energy do not qualify. Fourth, Fefferman notes the same questions are open and important for Euler ($\nu=0$), but Euler is not the prize problem — a fact that matters in §6, because the recent spectacular blowup results are for Euler-type dynamics, not Navier–Stokes.

3What the equation gives you for free

3.1 The energy identity — derived

Take (1) with $f\equiv 0$, dot with $u$, integrate over space. Two miracles of structure make the dangerous terms vanish. The transport term dies because a divergence-free field advects its own kinetic energy without creating any:

$$\int (u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot u \,dx=\tfrac12\int u\cdot\nabla|u|^2 \,dx=-\tfrac12\int (\operatorname{div}u)\,|u|^2 \,dx=0,$$

and the pressure term dies because pressure is a constraint force, orthogonal to the constraint surface: $\int \nabla p\cdot u = -\int p\,\operatorname{div}u = 0$. What survives is the energy identity

$$\frac{d}{dt}\,\frac12\int_{\mathbb{R}^3}|u|^2\,dx \;=\; -\,\nu\int_{\mathbb{R}^3}|\nabla u|^2\,dx\;\le\;0.\tag{E}$$

Kinetic energy only ever dissipates. Integrating in time: $\frac12\|u(t)\|_{L^2}^2+\nu\int_0^t\|\nabla u(s)\|_{L^2}^2\,ds=\frac12\|u^\circ\|_{L^2}^2$. This single a-priori bound is, ninety-two years after Leray, still essentially the only globally coercive information known for large solutions. Leray [Leray34] used it in 1934 to build global weak solutions (defined against test functions, as in equations (12)–(13) of Fefferman's document); whether they stay smooth, or are even unique, is the problem.

3.2 The scaling group, and the whole problem in one sentence

Navier–Stokes has an exact two-parameter symmetry: if $(u,p)$ solves (1)–(2), so does

$$u_\lambda(x,t)=\lambda\,u(\lambda x,\lambda^2 t),\qquad p_\lambda(x,t)=\lambda^2 p(\lambda x,\lambda^2 t),\qquad \lambda\gt 0,$$

with the same viscosity. Zooming in on a hypothetical singularity ($\lambda\to\infty$) is a symmetry of the problem, so any quantity that hopes to prevent singularities must at minimum not shrink as you zoom. Compute how the natural norms transform (a one-line calculation each, evaluating at rescaled times):

The entire difficulty of the Millennium Problem is this table. Everything the equation hands you for free lives strictly below scaling; everything that would force smoothness lives at or above it; and the zoom that approaches a singularity drains the former to zero while you need the latter. In two dimensions the energy is exactly scale-invariant ($\|\lambda u(\lambda\cdot)\|_{L^2(\mathbb{R}^2)}=\|u\|_{L^2(\mathbb{R}^2)}$), the free quantity is the critical quantity, and global regularity follows — that is Ladyzhenskaya's classical theorem [Lad], and it is why, as Fefferman says, dimension two "gives no hint" about dimension three. The 3D problem is called supercritical for exactly this reason, and no one has ever proven global regularity for any supercritical fluid-type PDE by estimates alone.

3.3 Vorticity: where the fight actually happens

Taking the curl of (1) eliminates pressure. With $\omega=\nabla\times u$:

$$\partial_t\omega+(u\cdot\nabla)\,\omega \;=\; (\omega\cdot\nabla)\,u+\nu\Delta\omega. \tag{V}$$

The term $(\omega\cdot\nabla)u$ is vortex stretching: velocity gradients amplify vorticity, and vorticity generates velocity gradients through the Biot–Savart law $\nabla u = \nabla\,(\nabla\times)(-\Delta)^{-1}\omega$, a zeroth-order singular integral. Schematically the vorticity magnitude obeys $\dot\Omega \sim \Omega^2$ — a Riccati equation, with guaranteed finite-time blowup if the schematic were the whole truth. In 2D the stretching term is identically zero ($\omega$ is a scalar transported and diffused, obeying a maximum principle) — the second, equivalent reason 2D is easy. In 3D everything known about possible blowup is a fight between quadratic self-amplification, transport (which relocates but does not amplify), and viscous dissipation. Section 8 exhibits this fight in a model where it can be resolved exactly.

4The standard machinery, pushed to its edge

This section does with full derivations what surveys do with citations, because I want to show where each estimate saturates, not just that it does.

4.1 The enstrophy Riccati inequality, and exactly what it buys

Let $X(t)=\|\omega(t)\|_{L^2}^2$ (enstrophy; recall $\|\omega\|_{L^2}=\|\nabla u\|_{L^2}$ for divergence-free fields, by expanding $|\nabla u|^2 = |\text{curl}\,u|^2 + (\operatorname{div} u)^2$ under the integral). Multiply (V) by $\omega$ and integrate; transport vanishes as in §3.1, leaving

$$\tfrac12 X'(t) \;=\; \int \omega\cdot S\,\omega\,dx \;-\;\nu\,\|\nabla\omega\|_{L^2}^2, \qquad S=\tfrac12\big(\nabla u+\nabla u^{\top}\big).$$

Estimate the stretching integral by Hölder, the Biot–Savart bound $\|S\|_{L^2}\le\|\nabla u\|_{L^2}=X^{1/2}$, and the 3D Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality $\|\omega\|_{L^4}\le C\|\omega\|_{L^2}^{1/4}\|\nabla\omega\|_{L^2}^{3/4}$:

$$\Big|\int \omega\cdot S\,\omega\Big| \;\le\; \|S\|_{L^2}\,\|\omega\|_{L^4}^2 \;\le\; C\,X^{1/2}\cdot X^{1/4}\,\|\nabla\omega\|_{L^2}^{3/2} \;\le\; \frac{\nu}{2}\,\|\nabla\omega\|_{L^2}^2 \;+\; \frac{C}{\nu^{3}}\,X^{3},$$

using Young's inequality with exponents $(4/3,4)$ in the last step. Therefore

$$X'(t)\;\le\;-\,\nu\,\|\nabla\omega\|_{L^2}^2\;+\;\frac{2C}{\nu^{3}}\,X(t)^{3}.\tag{R}$$

Dropping the good term and solving the Riccati inequality: $X(t)\le X_0\big(1-\tfrac{4C}{\nu^3}X_0^2\,t\big)^{-1/2}$. Three real theorems fall out of (R), and then it dies:

Local existence with an explicit time. Smoothness persists at least until $t\sim \nu^3/X_0^2$ — this is essentially Leray's local well-posedness, and it is why "blowup, if any, takes a while" is quantitative.

Leray's blowup rate. Reading (R) backwards: if $T$ is a first singular time, then necessarily $X(t)\;\ge\; c\,\nu^{3/2}(T-t)^{-1/2}$ — enstrophy must blow up at least this fast. Note the trap: the energy identity gives the budget $\int_0^\infty X(t)\,dt\le E_0/\nu$, and $\int^T (T-t)^{-1/2}dt$ converges. The only free global bound is arithmetically incapable of contradicting the mandatory blowup rate — the borderline case is exactly the self-similar rate, and the budget accommodates it with room to spare. This is supercriticality made concrete: the two known facts fit together perfectly on both the "smooth forever" and the "blows up" side of the ledger.

Global regularity for small data — derived. Interpolating $X=\|\nabla u\|_{L^2}^2\le\|u\|_{L^2}\|\Delta u\|_{L^2}=\|u\|_{L^2}\,\|\nabla\omega\|_{L^2}$ (integrate by parts, Cauchy–Schwarz; $\|\Delta u\|_{L^2}=\|\nabla\omega\|_{L^2}$ for divergence-free fields), the stretching bound $CX^{3/4}\|\nabla\omega\|^{3/2}_{L^2}$ becomes $\le C\,X^{1/4}\,\|u\|_{L^2}^{1/2}\,\|\nabla\omega\|_{L^2}^{2}$, so

$$X'\;\le\;\|\nabla\omega\|_{L^2}^2\,\Big(C\,\|u_0\|_{L^2}^{1/2}\,X^{1/4}-\nu\Big),$$

using $\|u(t)\|_{L^2}\le\|u_0\|_{L^2}$ from (E). If the data satisfy $C\|u_0\|_{L^2}^{1/2}\|\omega_0\|_{L^2}^{1/2}\lt \nu$ — i.e. $\|u_0\|_{L^2}\|\nabla u_0\|_{L^2}\lesssim\nu^2$, a scale-invariant smallness — then $X'\le 0$, the condition propagates, enstrophy never grows, and the solution is smooth forever. This is the classical Leray-type small-data theorem, and it is statement (A) proved — for data small enough. Modern theory pushes the smallness to the optimal critical spaces: Fujita–Kato ($\dot H^{1/2}$), Kato ($L^3$) [Kato84], and at the far end Koch–Tataru ($BMO^{-1}$, the largest space known) [KT01]; beyond it, in $\dot B^{-1}_{\infty,\infty}$, the problem becomes genuinely ill-posed (norm inflation, Bourgain–Pavlović [BP08]). The wall: every one of these arguments beats the quadratic term with the linear heat flow, which is possible precisely when a critical norm is small. For large data the same numerology returns with the opposite sign, from every known angle of attack.

The incompressible pressure constraint is doing real work in keeping the prize problem open.

4.3 The $L^3$ balance: the whole difficulty in one line

Why can't one just track the critical norm itself? Compute its evolution (multiply (1) by $u|u|$, integrate; the transport term vanishes as always):

$$\frac{d}{dt}\,\frac13\int|u|^3\,dx \;=\; -\,\nu\int |u|\,\Big(|\nabla u|^2+\big|\nabla|u|\big|^2\Big)\,dx\;+\;\int p\;u\cdot\nabla |u| \,dx.$$

The viscous term is beautifully signed. Everything hinges on the last term — the pressure flux. And the pressure is slaved to the velocity through $-\Delta p=\sum_{ij}\partial_i\partial_j(u_iu_j)$, i.e. $p=\sum_{ij}R_iR_j(u_iu_j)$, a nonlocal, sign-indefinite quadratic functional (Riesz transforms) — pressure at a point knows the flow everywhere. Every naive monotonicity argument for Navier–Stokes dies at this term, in this integral or one of its cousins. The known exceptions are exactly the special regimes where the pressure term can be tamed: small data (§4.1), two dimensions, axisymmetry without swirl (global regularity — Ladyzhenskaya; Ukhovskii–Yudovich), geometric depletion hypotheses [CF93]. I spent a real part of this session staring at this term from several angles; §7 records what I tried and how each attempt failed. (An explicit smooth periodic field for which the pressure term is not merely indefinite but wins — the critical $L^3$ mass instantaneously increasing at full viscous dissipation — is exhibited and machine-verified in Addendum A.1.) I am in large company: nobody has found a coercive critical quantity for 3D Navier–Stokes, and §5.1 gives the modern reason to think none of the standard kind exists.

5Three structural barriers

The results in this section are why "work harder with the same tools" is not a plan — they are theorems about the space of possible proofs.

5.1 Tao's averaged equation: the no-go theorem for soft arguments

In 2014 (published JAMS 2016 [Tao16]), Tao constructed an averaged Navier–Stokes equation: the true bilinear nonlinearity $B(u,u)$ is replaced by a carefully chosen average $\tilde B(u,u)$ of rotations and Fourier-localizations of itself. The averaged operator retains everything the standard toolkit ever uses — the same cancellation $\int \tilde B(u,u)\cdot u=0$ (hence the identical energy identity (E)), and the same harmonic-analysis estimates (Hölder, Sobolev, Bernstein bounds). And for this equation Tao proved finite-time blowup from smooth, finite-energy data — the solution organizes itself into a self-replicating cascade, a von Neumann machine of eddies that transfers its energy to ever-smaller scales in ever-shorter times.

The consequence is a precise no-go: any proof of (A)/(B) that uses only the energy identity plus generic function-space estimates would apply equally to the averaged equation, and is therefore wrong. A successful regularity argument must see finer structure of the actual nonlinearity — the specific geometry that averaging destroys. Nothing in the standard arsenal does. This is the modern, rigorous form of the folklore that supercritical problems cannot be closed by estimates, and it is the reason §7's failed attempts were guaranteed to fail in the way they failed. Tao has also drawn the constructive moral: if one could build such a self-replicating machine out of the true Euler/Navier–Stokes dynamics, one would prove (C)/(D). That reading is an ancestor of the entire program in §6.

5.2 No self-similar exit

The oldest concrete blowup proposal is Leray's own (1934): seek $u(x,t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2a(T-t)}}\,U\!\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{2a(T-t)}}\Big)$, reducing blowup to a profile equation for $U$. Sixty-two years later, Nečas–Růžička–Šverák [NRS96] closed the door: any profile $U\in L^3(\mathbb{R}^3)$ is identically zero; Tsai [Tsai98] extended the exclusion to profiles with locally finite natural energy. So Navier–Stokes singularities, if they exist, are not exactly self-similar in any reasonable class: they must be nearly self-similar with drifting corrections, discretely self-similar, or genuinely unstable — categorically harder to construct and to certify. (Note how the fine print of §2 bites: the bounded-energy condition (7) is exactly the kind of constraint these exclusion theorems weaponize.) When people say the problem "repels its own most natural solution mechanisms," this is what they mean. The frontier program of §6 is best understood as the systematic response: if stable self-similarity is forbidden, hunt unstable singularities with enough numerical precision to certify them anyway.

5.3 The equation is wild just below smoothness

The third barrier is a body of results showing that everything around the smooth theory misbehaves, so any proof of good behavior must lean on smoothness itself — there is no soft compactness or selection principle waiting to help. Fefferman's document already cites the ancestors: Scheffer and Shnirelman's Euler flows, compactly supported in spacetime — fluid that starts at rest, spontaneously moves, and returns to rest [Sch93, Shn97]. The modern convex-integration revolution made this generic: Isett settled the Onsager conjecture for Euler [Isett18], and Buckmaster–Vicol proved that distributional Navier–Stokes solutions with finite kinetic energy are flexible — the same data admit many solutions [BV19]. That still left Leray's own class (weak solutions obeying the energy inequality) as a possible refuge. It has fallen in stages: Jia–Šverák built the conditional scenario [JS14]; Albritton–Brué–Colombo proved nonuniqueness of Leray–Hopf solutions with a force (Annals 2022 [ABC22]), seeded by Vishik's unstable vortex; and in September 2025, Hou–Wang–Yang posted a rigorous computer-assisted proof of nonuniqueness for the unforced equation [HWY25]: infinitely many suitable Leray–Hopf solutions emanating from a single divergence-free initial datum of the scale-invariant form $u^\circ(x)=|x|^{-1}A(x/|x|)$ — finite energy, but singular at one point, sitting just outside the Prodi–Serrin uniqueness class ($u^\circ\in L^q$ for every $q\lt 3$, and $L^3$ fails exactly). The mechanism is an unstable eigenvalue of the linearization around a self-similar solution, certified by rigorous numerics; the preprint (revised March 2026) is still under review, and its computer-assisted core will take the community time to audit — but it is exactly the shape of result the field expected, and its toolchain matters for §6.

None of these theorems touches (A)–(D): they all live at data rougher than the Schwartz-class smoothness the prize demands, and nonuniqueness is a different failure than nonexistence. What they establish is the direction of the wind. Well-posedness for 3D Navier–Stokes is not a robust phenomenon degrading gracefully at the edges; it is a smooth island in a genuinely wild sea, and the water is at $L^3$'s doorstep. (Second-session update: it is now past the doorstep — see Addendum A.2 for the 2025–26 results I had missed: nonuniqueness of solutions that are smooth for positive time from critical data (Coiculescu–Palasek, Inventiones 2025), and, from smooth data, a weak branch that departs from the classical one and undergoes instantaneous Type-I blowup — while the classical branch survives, so the Clay statements still stand untouched.)

6The live frontier (2025–2026)

If the problem falls in the foreseeable future, current evidence says it falls in the breakdown direction, through a research program that has been climbing a ladder of increasingly hard singularity constructions for a decade. The rungs, in order:

Numerical scenario (2014). Luo–Hou's landmark computation [HL14]: axisymmetric Euler flow in a cylinder, vorticity amplification by $10^8$ at a boundary ring, cleanly (nearly) self-similar — the first blowup candidate credible enough to organize a decade of analysis. Low-regularity proof (2021). Elgindi [Elg21] proved finite-time singularity for 3D axisymmetric Euler on $\mathbb{R}^3$ with $C^{1,\alpha}$ (not smooth) velocity — showing the mechanism is real, at the price of weakened regularity. Smooth-data proof (2022–25). Chen–Hou [CH22] proved stable, nearly self-similar blowup for 2D Boussinesq and 3D axisymmetric Euler with smooth data and boundary — the Luo–Hou scenario made rigorous. The proof is a hybrid: hundreds of pages of energy estimates around a numerically computed approximate profile, with the residual spectral bounds certified by interval arithmetic; the announcement layer appeared in PNAS in 2025 [CH25] with the rigorous-numerics part in MMS [CH24]. This is the current world record in certified blowup, and the template for everything below. Unstable profiles at scale (2025). The DeepMind collaboration with Brown/NYU/Stanford mathematicians ("Discovery of Unstable Singularities," arXiv September 2025 [DM25], blog) used physics-informed neural networks driven to near machine precision to find whole families of unstable self-similar blowup profiles for the incompressible porous media equation and for 3D Euler with boundary (equivalently, 2D Boussinesq) — including an unexpected empirical law: blowup rate $\lambda$ varies linearly with the order of instability, a pattern predicting further profiles. Unstable singularities are precisely what §5.2 says Navier–Stokes requires, and precisely what ordinary numerics cannot see (any discretization error falls off the unstable manifold); the stated ambition of that line of work is profiles accurate enough to feed Chen–Hou-style computer-assisted proofs. Certified unstable NS object (2025–26). Hou–Wang–Yang's unforced nonuniqueness result [HWY25] is the first rigorous, computer-assisted certification of an unstable self-similar structure for 3D Navier–Stokes itself — used there to break uniqueness rather than smoothness, from singular rather than smooth data. Every tool a (C)/(D) proof needs now exists somewhere in this stack; no single result yet composes them.

What still separates this program from the prize: (i) the ansatz gap — for NS blowup, exact self-similarity is forbidden (§5.2), so one needs an admissible nearly/unstably self-similar ansatz in which viscosity, which is exactly marginal under the blowup scaling (it enters the rescaled profile equation at order one, unlike for Euler scenarios where it is absent), is fought to a standstill rather than allowed to smooth the singularity away; no such NS ansatz with a computable profile is currently known. (ii) the boundary gap — Chen–Hou's mechanism leans on a solid boundary; statements (C)/(D) live on $\mathbb{R}^3$ with decay, or on the boundaryless torus. (iii) the certificate gap — certifying an unstable blowup means rigorously controlling all unstable directions while shooting onto the stable manifold with data that must also satisfy (4)/(8); this has been done for model equations, not for NS. On the regularity side, by contrast, there is no comparable engine: quantitative regularity inches forward (Tao [Tao19], Palasek [Palasek], and 2026 forced-equation refinements), and the honest summary of expert opinion is a split — with a widely shared lean, following the averaged-equation evidence, that Euler-type blowup is real and that for Navier–Stokes the smart money is divided and unhurried. Nobody serious is predicting a resolution this year; several serious people are no longer predicting "not in my lifetime."

A1. Hunting a coercive critical quantity

The dream: a functional $\Phi(u(t))$, (i) at or above critical scaling, (ii) bounded a priori under the flow, (iii) strong enough that its boundedness implies regularity (via §4.2's ladder). Candidates and verdicts. Energy: fails (i) — supercritical, §3.2. Enstrophy: fails (ii) — its inequality (R) is exactly the one that permits blowup. Helicity $\int u\cdot\omega\,dx$: passes (i) — it is scale-invariant — but fails (ii) and (iii): under Navier–Stokes $\frac{d}{dt}\int u\cdot\omega = -2\nu\int \nabla u:\nabla\omega$, sign-indefinite, and helicity bounds no norm (it is a signed, cancelling quantity; large antiparallel vortex pairs have huge motion and zero helicity). Vorticity mass $\|\omega\|_{L^1}$: fails (i) (scales like $\lambda^{-1}$) and (ii) (vortex stretching defeats the max principle that makes this work in 2D). The critical norm itself $\|u\|_{L^3}^3$: passes (i) by definition, fails (ii) at exactly one term — the pressure flux $\int p\,u\cdot\nabla|u|$, §4.3, sign-indefinite and nonlocal. I tried the usual reorganizations (Bernoulli variable $p+\tfrac12|u|^2$; splitting $p$ by frequency; positive-part truncations à la De Giorgi on level sets of $|u|$) and each reintroduces the same indefinite commutator at critical order. Fourier-side monotonicity (a signed "flux across scales"): the convolution $\widehat{u\otimes u}$ moves energy both up and down the spectrum with no definite sign in any weighting I could find that also survives (ii); this is the standard Littlewood–Paley dead end, and Tao's theorem says a weighting cannot exist unless it detects the un-averaged structure of $B$. Maximum principles: $\tfrac12|u|^2$ obeys $\,(\partial_t+u\cdot\nabla-\nu\Delta)\tfrac12|u|^2=-\nu|\nabla u|^2-u\cdot\nabla p$ — ruined by the pressure gradient; $|\omega|$ is ruined by stretching. A2. Trying to cheat statements (C)/(D)

The tempting shortcut: choose a field $u(x,t)$ that blows up at time $T$ by construction, then define the force as the residual $f:=\partial_t u+(u\cdot\nabla)u-\nu\Delta u+\nabla p$, so that $(u,p,f)$ solves (1) tautologically. This fails the fine print instantly: condition (5)/(9) demands $f$ smooth and decaying on all of $[0,\infty)$, but the residual of a singular $u$ inherits the singularity at $T$ (if it did not, $u$ would be a smooth solution of a smoothly forced equation and — by local theory — could not have blown up). The force is not allowed to carry the singularity; the nonlinearity must generate it autonomously while the force stays asleep. I also checked the dual cheat — manufacturing nonexistence without blowup — and it is unavailable: for smooth admissible data, local existence and uniqueness in the smooth class are classical, so "no solution on $[0,\infty)$" can only mean "the unique local solution has finite maximal time." 8A blowup you can hold in your hand

To make the stretching-versus-everything fight of §3.3 concrete, here is the one place it can be resolved exactly: the Constantin–Lax–Majda model [CLM85]. Strip (V) of transport and pressure, keep only stretching, and model the Biot–Savart coupling by the Hilbert transform $H$ (the canonical zeroth-order singular integral in 1D, $\widehat{Hf}(k)=-i\,\mathrm{sgn}(k)\hat f(k)$):

$$\partial_t\omega \;=\; \omega\, H\omega. \tag{CLM}$$

This looks hopeless — nonlinear, nonlocal — until you notice the complex structure. Set $z=H\omega+i\,\omega$. The Cotlar-type identity $H(\omega H\omega)=\tfrac12\big((H\omega)^2-\omega^2\big)$ (I verified it numerically to $9\times10^{-15}$ on random data) turns (CLM) into a pointwise Riccati ODE:

$$\partial_t z \;=\; \partial_t(H\omega) + i\,\partial_t\omega \;=\; \tfrac12\big((H\omega)^2-\omega^2\big) + i\,\omega H\omega \;=\;\tfrac12 z^2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad z(x,t)=\frac{z_0(x)}{1-\tfrac{t}{2}z_0(x)},$$

whose imaginary part gives the exact solution

$$\omega(x,t)\;=\;\frac{\omega_0(x)}{\big(1-\tfrac{t}{2}H\omega_0(x)\big)^2+\tfrac{t^2}{4}\,\omega_0(x)^2}.$$

The solution blows up in finite time if and only if some point has $\omega_0(x)=0$ with $H\omega_0(x)\gt 0$, at time $T=2/\max\{H\omega_0(x):\omega_0(x)=0\}$. For $\omega_0=\sin x$ on the circle, $H\omega_0=-\cos x$, so the singularity forms at $x=\pi$ at time $T=2$, and a short computation gives $\max_x|\omega|\sim (T-t)^{-1}$ — precisely the Beale–Kato–Majda borderline rate, with $\int^T\!\sup_x|\omega|\,dt$ diverging logarithmically, as any true vorticity blowup must. I verified the exact solution against the PDE spectrally (residual $\lt 10^{-12}$ at all tested times) and confirmed $(T-t)\max|\omega|\to 1$:

Why show this in a document about Navier–Stokes? Because it calibrates the whole subject. Vortex stretching, left alone, certainly produces finite-time singularities — that has been beyond doubt since 1985; the Millennium question is only whether 3D's specific chaperones suppress it. And the model family tells you how delicate the chaperones are: add viscosity to (CLM) and explicit solutions still blow up (Schochet [Sch86] — dissipation alone does not save you); add back transport (De Gregorio's modification [DG90]) and the answer splits by regularity — global well-posedness near equilibrium (Jia–Stewart–Šverák [JSS19]) and for smooth circle data (conjecturally), yet self-similar blowup for low-regularity data (Chen–Hou–Huang [CHH21]; see also [CMP23]), with dissipative variants exhibiting a genuine phase diagram between the two [gCLM]. Transport and dissipation are each real defenses, each breachable. In the full 3D equation, stretching, transport, and viscosity enter at exactly equal scaling weight — the marginality of §6(i) — so no term wins on dimensional grounds, and the outcome turns on constants and geometry that ninety years of analysis have not pinned down. That is the honest content of "supercritical": the equation sits on the knife edge that every simplified relative falls off, one way or the other.

B.1 Audit of v2: the Type-II window — verified, and my own §6 corrected

Version 2's main addition corrects a claim in this document. My §6(i) said viscosity is "exactly marginal under the blowup scaling." That is true at the parabolic (Type-I) scale and only there. Write $s=T-t$, $\tau=-\log s$, and rescale with a free similarity exponent $\gamma$: $u(x,t)=s^{\gamma-1}\,U\!\big(\tfrac{x-x_*}{s^{\gamma}},\tau\big)$, $p=s^{2\gamma-2}P$. B.2 Attack: a chirality-balance identity for critical-norm growth (proved and machine-verified)

Decompose $u$ on $\mathbb{T}^3$ into helical modes: $\hat u(k)=a_+(k)h_+(k)+a_-(k)h_-(k)$, where $ik\times h_\pm=\pm|k|h_\pm$. Write $H^\pm=\sum_k |k|\,|a_\pm(k)|^2$, so the critical ($\dot H^{1/2}$) content is $\mathcal{C}=H^++H^-$ and the helicity is $H=H^+-H^-$. The nonlinearity conserves helicity; the viscous term is diagonal in this basis. Hence, denoting by $(\cdot)_{\mathrm{nl}}$ the contribution of the nonlinear term,

$$\Big(\tfrac{d}{dt}\mathcal{C}\Big)_{\mathrm{nl}}\;=\;2\Big(\tfrac{d}{dt}H^+\Big)_{\mathrm{nl}}\;=\;2\Big(\tfrac{d}{dt}H^-\Big)_{\mathrm{nl}} .$$

Proof: $(dH/dt)_{\mathrm{nl}}=0$ gives $(dH^+)_{\mathrm{nl}}=(dH^-)_{\mathrm{nl}}$; add them. I verified this spectrally on random divergence-free fields: the three quantities agree to every displayed digit (relative agreement $\sim10^{-16}$), with the energy check $(dE/dt)_{\mathrm{nl}}\sim10^{-19}$. The identity is elementary — it is implicit in why Biferale–Titi's decimated system is regular, and their theorem is its one-line corollary (single chirality $\Rightarrow H^-\equiv0\Rightarrow(\dot{\mathcal C})_{\mathrm{nl}}=0\Rightarrow$ the critical norm never grows $\Rightarrow$ global regularity). I make no novelty claim. Its value here is what it says about the module problem of Addendum A.3: any singular cascade must grow the critical content of both chiralities at identically equal nonlinear rates, at every instant. A blowup cannot be built helically "in place"; it must continuously manufacture the minority chirality at half its total critical production. That converts the note's qualitative "heterochiral leakage" clause into an exact global constraint — and B.3–B.4 measure its local cost.
