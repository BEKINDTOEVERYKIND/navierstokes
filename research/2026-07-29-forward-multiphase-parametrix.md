# Forward multiphase evolution: no finite-band cokernel and a single-parent rank-five repair

## Status

This note directly tests the forward-in-time replacement for the static
cell inverse in the three-beat construction.  It does **not** prove a
Navier--Stokes singularity.

The conclusions are sharper than either “forward evolution fixes the
problem” or “the infinite cokernel kills the construction.”

1. On every finite charge/harmonic band, the complete multiphase Kelvin
   system is a globally defined forward Galerkin ODE.  Its source problem
   with incoming data prescribed has no cokernel.  Backward heat on the
   retained band costs only
   \(\exp(C\theta M^2)\), which tends to one in the polynomial-carrier
   ledger.
2. This functional-analytic fact is not a scale-uniform endpoint theorem.
   The generic variational bound is
   \(\exp(CMKT)\), and its order-\(m\) parameter derivatives have an
   explicit Gevrey-1 majorant with base \(CMK\).  On an order-one stage
   this is incompatible with the planned
   \(K_j=j^A,\ A>2\), truncation.
3. The explicit three-coordinate-colour design has an exact
   transparency-versus-rank obstruction.  Cancelling every leading
   \(O(K)\) cross-colour interaction forces a nondegenerate leading bath
   to have one common helicity, hence to be Beltrami.  A homochiral
   near-opposite beat has only one real child polarization at leading
   order.  The three child-strain columns therefore have rank at most
   three, while the required chart has rank five.
4. There is a concrete repair of that principal obstruction.  Eight
   homochiral sidebands on one common integer shell give a full rank-eight
   chart of \(\mathfrak{sl}(3)\), with exact determinant \(-3/10\).
   Six of the eight synthesize
   \(\operatorname{diag}(-1,-5/4,9/4)\) exactly.  Every base wave number
   has magnitude \(K\sqrt2+O(1)\), so the homochiral identity bounds
   **every** base cross interaction by \(O(1)\), not \(O(K)\).
5. The six nonzero target beats can all use the **same** fast direction.
   In that one-charge subsystem, incompressibility cancels \(K\) from
   every charged--charged interaction exactly, not only at the initial
   shell.  After low-mode transport is put into the material phase, the
   order-\(m\) interaction coefficient is \(O(m^2)\), which closes a
   Gevrey-2 majorant \(C^m(m!)^2\).  The price is one missing endpoint
   coordinate: a fixed fast direction spans a rank-seven hyperplane in
   \(\mathfrak{sl}(3)\).
6. More efficiently, three arbitrary-polarization partners can share the
   single parent \(k=Ke_3,\ a=e_1\).  For the original three low
   directions, each exact finite-\(K\) child map has rank two and the
   combined strain chart has rank five.  The leading fast bath is an
   arbitrary transverse one-phase Euler shear, so the all-chain
   \(K\)-cancellation does not require homochirality.

The repair is a genuine new principal parametrix, but not the missing
return theorem.  The two-fast-direction rank-eight chart still has an
off-shell semigroup problem.  The one-fast-direction substar avoids that
specific \(K\)-stiffness but leaves one endpoint coordinate plus the
low-flow, localization, viscosity, and physical wake-routing problems.
The arbitrary-polarization three-partner version restores the needed
rank-five strain chart; it is the preferred target below.

All rational and complex-vector identities below are checked in
[`checks/forward_multiphase_parametrix.py`](../checks/forward_multiphase_parametrix.py).

## 1. Exact charge coordinates for the published three beats

Let the three low directions be

\[
\begin{aligned}
q_1&=(-45,-36,20),\\
q_2&=(-4,-5,9),\\
q_3&=(1,1,1),
\end{aligned}
\tag{1.1}
\]

and let \(Q\) be the matrix with these columns.  Exact arithmetic gives

\[
\det Q=182.
\tag{1.2}
\]

For the simplest explicit high directions, put

\[
k_i=K e_i,\qquad l_i=q_i-Ke_i.
\tag{1.3}
\]

Every Fourier mode generated by the six real waves has a unique formal
label

\[
\alpha=(h,n)\in\mathbb Z^3\times\mathbb Z^3,
\qquad
p_K(\alpha)=Kh+Qn.
\tag{1.4}
\]

The parent and partner labels are

\[
k_i:(e_i,0),\qquad l_i:(-e_i,e_i).
\tag{1.5}
\]

The charge is \(h\).  Quadratic convolution is simply

\[
(h,n)+(g,m)=(h+g,n+m).
\tag{1.6}
\]

Use the finite band

\[
{\cal I}_M
=
\{(h,n):|h|_1+|n|_1\le M\}.
\tag{1.7}
\]

If two labels in \({\cal I}_M\) give the same physical wave number, then

\[
K(h-h')=-Q(n-n').
\tag{1.8}
\]

Each coordinate on the right has absolute value at most \(90M\).  Hence

\[
\boxed{K>90M\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
       \alpha\mapsto p_K(\alpha)\ \hbox{is injective on }{\cal I}_M.}
\tag{1.9}
\]

If \(h=h'\), (1.2) gives \(n=n'\); if not, one coordinate on the left of
(1.8) has size at least \(K\), contradicting the bound.

Moreover,

\[
|p_K(h,n)|
\le (K+62)M.
\tag{1.10}
\]

Thus an order-\(M\) charged construction produces physical harmonics
\(O(MK)\), exactly the growth assumed in the flatness ledger.  This is a
support statement, not yet a norm estimate.

## 2. Exact finite-band Kelvin system, including viscosity

Take the symmetric affine strain

\[
S=\operatorname{diag}\left(-1,-\frac54,\frac94\right).
\tag{2.1}
\]

For every label \(\alpha\), let the material wave vector be

\[
\kappa_\alpha(t)=e^{-S^Tt}p_K(\alpha).
\tag{2.2}
\]

It obeys

\[
\kappa_\alpha'=-S^T\kappa_\alpha,
\qquad
\kappa_{\alpha+\beta}
=\kappa_\alpha+\kappa_\beta.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Consider the finite Kelvin expansion

\[
u(x,t)=Sx+
\sum_{\alpha\in{\cal I}_M\setminus\{0\}}
A_\alpha(t)e^{i\kappa_\alpha(t)\cdot x},
\tag{2.4}
\]

with

\[
\kappa_\alpha\cdot A_\alpha=0,
\qquad
A_{-\alpha}=\overline{A_\alpha}.
\tag{2.5}
\]

Projecting products back to \({\cal I}_M\) gives the exact Galerkin
amplitude equations

\[
\begin{aligned}
A_\alpha'
={}&-SA_\alpha
+2\kappa_\alpha
 \frac{\kappa_\alpha\cdot SA_\alpha}{|\kappa_\alpha|^2}
-\varepsilon|\kappa_\alpha|^2A_\alpha\\
&-i\mathbb P_{\kappa_\alpha}
\sum_{\substack{\beta+\gamma=\alpha\\
                 \beta,\gamma\in{\cal I}_M}}
(A_\beta\cdot\kappa_\gamma)A_\gamma .
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.6}
\]

Here \(\varepsilon\) is the normalized viscosity.  The second term in the
first line is the pressure correction required to preserve
\(\kappa_\alpha\cdot A_\alpha=0\).  Equation (2.6) retains every charge
chain in the chosen band.  Its omitted Fourier coefficients are precisely
the finite-band tail, not silently discarded “resonances.”

The nonlinear term has the usual Galerkin energy cancellation.  Taking
the real part of the \(\ell^2\) pairing with \(A\) gives

\[
\frac12\frac d{dt}\sum_\alpha|A_\alpha|^2
+\varepsilon\sum_\alpha|\kappa_\alpha|^2|A_\alpha|^2
=-\sum_\alpha\operatorname{Re}
  \big(\overline{A_\alpha}\cdot SA_\alpha\big).
\tag{2.7}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\|A(t)\|_{\ell^2}
\le e^{\|S\|t}\|A(0)\|_{\ell^2}.
\tag{2.8}
\]

The finite-dimensional forward system is global.  The estimate is
independent of \(K,M,\varepsilon\).

### Proposition 2.1: the forward finite-band operator has no cokernel

Linearize (2.6) about any solution \(A(t)\):

\[
Z'={\cal L}_{K,M}(t)Z+G,\qquad Z(0)=Z_{\rm in}.
\tag{2.9}
\]

For every \(Z_{\rm in}\) and integrable \(G\), variation of constants gives
a unique \(Z\).  In particular, on the domain \(Z(0)=0\), the map

\[
Z\longmapsto Z'-{\cal L}_{K,M}(t)Z
\tag{2.10}
\]

is bijective onto the finite-band source space.  There are no adjoint
moment conditions.  The infinite carrier-chain cokernel of the static
operator has become ordinary outgoing state data.

If the complete finite-band terminal state is prescribed instead, the
finite-dimensional heat inverse contributes at most

\[
\exp\left(
\varepsilon\int_0^T
\max_{\alpha\in{\cal I}_M}|\kappa_\alpha(t)|^2\,dt
\right)
\le
\exp(C_T\theta M^2),
\qquad
\theta=\varepsilon K^2.
\tag{2.11}
\]

In the polynomial-carrier regime,

\[
\theta_jM_j^2\longrightarrow0.
\tag{2.12}
\]

Thus viscosity does not recreate the infinite cokernel on the retained
band.  It does make an all-harmonic backward inverse impossible, exactly
as in the one-phase audit.

## 3. What forward solvability does not give: the Gevrey majorant

Use the coefficient \(\ell^1\) norm, for which convolution is an algebra.
On a ball \(\|A\|_{\ell^1}\le R\), (1.10) gives

\[
\|D{\cal N}_{K,M}(A)\|
\le C_TMK R.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Ignoring the favorable sign of forward heat, set

\[
\Lambda=C_T(1+MKR).
\tag{3.2}
\]

The standard analytic-ODE majorant for the flow \(\Phi_T\) of a quadratic
vector field yields

\[
\boxed{
\|D^m\Phi_T(A_{\rm in})\|
\le
m!\,e^{\Lambda T}
\left(C_TMK\,T\,e^{\Lambda T}\right)^{m-1}.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

This can also be proved directly by differentiating the ODE \(m\) times,
using the binomial convolution of the lower derivatives, and comparing
with the scalar Riccati majorant.  It is a Gevrey-1/factorial bound, but
its base is not uniform.

The same bookkeeping shows that the \(m\)-th Picard or WKB interaction
created from the base carriers has charge size \(O(m)\), hence wave number
\(O(mK)\).  Truncation at interaction order \(M\) gives the promised
\(O(MK)\) harmonic support and, without further structure, the
\(\exp(CMKT)\) variational cost.

For

\[
K_j=j^A,\qquad
M_j\asymp\frac{j^2}{\log j},\qquad A>2,
\tag{3.4}
\]

the generic order-one-stage estimate (3.3) is much larger than
\(e^{cj^2}\).  It therefore destroys an \(e^{-cj^2}\) truncation
remainder.  This is a rigorous obstruction to the **unstructured
forward-energy proof**, not proof that every specially prepared bath is
actually unstable.

On a pulse of length \(T\lesssim K^{-1}\), the cost improves to
\(\exp(CM)\), which is \(e^{o(j^2)}\).  But an order-one carrier amplitude
then creates only an \(O(K^{-1})\) low child.  Shortening the pulse alone
does not create the required order-one strain.

## 4. Small-time child rank and why it is not uniform

Let \({\cal C}\) project the charge-zero coefficients
\(\{A_{(0,e_i)}\}_{i=1}^3\) to the symmetric gradient at the origin.
The unequal-radius calculation in the two-colour note gives six real
carrier controls \(\xi\) for which the instantaneous matched symbol
satisfies

\[
\operatorname{rank}
D_\xi\left.\frac d{dt}{\cal C}(A(t))\right|_{t=0}
=5
\tag{4.1}
\]

for all sufficiently large \(K\), with a principal right inverse bounded
independently of \(K\).

For each fixed \(K,M\), Taylor's theorem then gives

\[
D_\xi{\cal C}(A(T))
=TJ_K+O(T^2MK).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Hence the finite-band endpoint map is onto for

\[
TMK\le c,
\tag{4.3}
\]

with right-inverse norm \(O(T^{-1})\).  This proves an actual forward
endpoint submersion, but only on the stiffness time scale.  It does not
prove a scale-uniform order-one return.

Affine material phases remove the \(O(K)\) transport by \(Sx\) exactly in
(2.2).  They do not remove \(O(K)\) high--high interactions between
different fast colours.  The latter are the obstruction analyzed next.

## 5. Exact transparency classification for three coordinate colours

Write the leading positive-frequency coefficients on the three coordinate
directions as

\[
A_1=(0,a,b),\qquad
A_2=(c,0,d),\qquad
A_3=(e,f,0).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Assume the six displayed scalar components are nonzero.  Requiring the
projected quadratic coefficients at every
\(e_i+e_j\) and \(e_i-e_j\) to vanish gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
ad+cb&=0,&-a\overline d+\overline c\,b&=0,\\
bf+ea&=0,&-b\overline f+\overline e\,a&=0,\\
de+fc&=0,&-d\overline e+\overline f\,c&=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Put \(r=b/a\).  The first row implies

\[
\frac dc=-r,\qquad r=-\overline r.
\tag{5.3}
\]

The second gives

\[
\frac fe=-\frac1r.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Substitution in the third gives

\[
r^2=-1.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Therefore

\[
r=\pm i,
\tag{5.6}
\]

and the three \(A_i\)'s have one common helicity.  Equivalently, the
leading real field is a curl eigenfunction:

\[
\operatorname{curl}_y W_0=\pm W_0.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Degenerate cases of (5.2) lose one or more polarization components and
cannot supply the published rank-five chart.  Thus, in the nondegenerate
three-coordinate setup, leading transparency forces a common-helicity
Beltrami bath.

The cost of leaving this set is real.  For example,

\[
A_1^+=(0,1,i),\qquad A_2^-=(1,0,i)
\tag{5.8}
\]

give at \(e_1+e_2\)

\[
\mathbb P_{e_1+e_2}
\big((A_1^+\cdot e_2)A_2^-
    (A_2^-\cdot e_1)A_1^+\big)
=(0,0,2i).
\tag{5.9}
\]

At physical wave number \(K(e_1+e_2)\), this is a genuine \(O(K)\)
unmatched interaction.

## 6. Homochiral beats lose one child polarization

Let \(p,r\) carry the same helicity \(s\in\{\pm1\}\):

\[
ip\times a=s|p|a,\qquad
ir\times b=s|r|b.
\tag{6.1}
\]

The identity

\[
(u\cdot\nabla)u
=\nabla\frac{|u|^2}{2}-u\times\operatorname{curl}u
\tag{6.2}
\]

gives the exact two-mode formula

\[
i{\cal B}_{p+r}(a,b)
=s(|p|-|r|)
\mathbb P_{p+r}(a\times b).
\tag{6.3}
\]

In particular,

\[
|{\cal B}_{p+r}(a,b)|
\le \big||p|-|r|\big|\,|a|\,|b|.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Take

\[
p=K w,\qquad r=q-Kw.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Then

\[
|p|-|r|
=\frac{w\cdot q}{|w|}+O(K^{-1}).
\tag{6.6}
\]

With phases chosen continuously as \(K\to\infty\),

\[
a\times b
\longrightarrow
\hbox{a nonzero imaginary scalar times }\frac w{|w|}.
\tag{6.7}
\]

Thus the leading real child polarization is the single line

\[
\boxed{c(w,q)=\mathbb P_qw.}
\tag{6.8}
\]

With unit helical vectors and the phase convention used in (6.7), the
actual amplitude-normalized symbol is

\[
{\cal B}_q
=-\frac{w\cdot q}{|w|^2}\,\mathbb P_qw+O(K^{-1}).
\tag{6.9}
\]

Amplitude and phase change the sine/cosine coefficient on that line; they
do not recover the second real direction in \(q^\perp\).

For the three published pairs \(w_i=e_i\), the three symmetric child
columns are

\[
\operatorname{sym}\big(c(e_i,q_i)\otimes q_i\big),
\qquad i=1,2,3.
\tag{6.10}
\]

Their exact rank is three.  Consequently,

\[
\boxed{\text{leading }O(K)\text{ transparency}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{child-strain rank }\le3<5.}
\tag{6.11}
\]

The full unequal-radius rank-two symbol is recovered only by a
heterochiral/transverse direction.  In the simultaneous three-colour
bath, such a direction leaves the transparent manifold and excites
terms of the form (5.9).

This is the promised decisive obstruction to the **explicit
three-coordinate-doublet parametrix**.  Forward evolution removes its
static cokernel, but it does not reconcile uniform rank five with leading
fast transparency.

## 7. A near-shell homochiral repair

The obstruction in Section 6 says to use more rank-one homochiral beats,
not to restore a stiff heterochiral direction.  The following integer data
do exactly that.

For integer \(K\), define

\[
k_a=Kw_a,\qquad l_a=q_a-Kw_a,
\qquad a=1,\ldots,8,
\tag{7.1}
\]

with

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
a&w_a&q_a&c_a=\mathbb P_{q_a}w_a\\ \hline
1&(1,1,0)&(-1,0,0)&(0,1,0)\\
2&(1,1,0)&(0,-1,0)&(1,0,0)\\
3&(1,1,0)&(-1,0,-1)&(1/2,1,-1/2)\\
4&(1,1,0)&(-1,0,1)&(1/2,1,1/2)\\
5&(1,1,0)&(0,-1,-1)&(1,1/2,-1/2)\\
6&(1,1,0)&(0,-1,1)&(1,1/2,1/2)\\
7&(1,1,0)&(-2,0,-1)&(1/5,1,-2/5)\\
8&(1,-1,0)&(-1,0,-1)&(1/2,-1,-1/2).
\end{array}
\tag{7.2}
\]

All leading high directions lie on one shell:

\[
|w_a|^2=2.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Moreover \(w_a\cdot q_a\ne0\), so every matched pair is unequal-radius.
The actual integer wave numbers satisfy

\[
\begin{aligned}
|k_a|&=K\sqrt2,\\
|l_a|^2
&=2K^2-2K(w_a\cdot q_a)+|q_a|^2,\\
|l_a|&=K\sqrt2-\frac{w_a\cdot q_a}{\sqrt2}
+O(K^{-1}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.4}
\]

Hence every member of

\[
{\cal S}_K
=\{\pm k_a,\pm l_a:1\le a\le8\}
\tag{7.5}
\]

has magnitude \(K\sqrt2+O(1)\), with an absolute constant independent of
\(K\).  In fact all the \(q_a\)'s in (7.2) satisfy
\(|q_a|\le\sqrt5\), so the reverse triangle inequality gives the explicit
bound

\[
\big||p|-K\sqrt2\big|\le\sqrt5
\qquad(p\in{\cal S}_K).
\tag{7.6}
\]

Give every positive-frequency mode the same helicity \(s\), and impose
reality by

\[
\widehat u(-p)=\overline{\widehat u(p)}.
\tag{7.7}
\]

The convention can be chosen so that

\[
h_{-p}^s=\overline{h_p^s}.
\tag{7.8}
\]

Thus conjugation preserves the same helicity sign.  Formula (6.4) applies
to **every pair**, including unmatched cross-family pairs and pairs
involving conjugate modes.  From (7.4),

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{p,r\in{\cal S}_K}
|{\cal B}_{p+r}(h_p^s,h_r^s)|
\le 2\sqrt5,
}
\tag{7.9}
\]

whereas an unprepared pair is only bounded by \(CK\).

If all radii in (7.5) were exactly equal, the entire real field would
satisfy

\[
\operatorname{curl}u=sK\sqrt2\,u
\tag{7.10}
\]

and every projected quadratic interaction would vanish.  Here the
\(O(1)\) norm splittings in (7.4) are exactly what create the low children
and the unmatched \(O(1)\) wake.

### 7.1 Exact full-gradient chart

Use the eight coordinates

\[
(M_{11},M_{22},M_{12},M_{13},M_{21},M_{23},M_{31},M_{32})
\tag{7.11}
\]

on \(\mathfrak{sl}(3)\), with
\(M_{33}=-M_{11}-M_{22}\).  Let

\[
C_a=c_a\otimes q_a.
\tag{7.12}
\]

Exact rational elimination gives

\[
\boxed{\det[C_1\ \cdots\ C_8]=-\frac3{10}.}
\tag{7.13}
\]

This is the determinant of the amplitude-normalized polarization columns.
The raw homochiral symbol (6.9) multiplies column \(a\) by
\[
g_a=-\frac{w_a\cdot q_a}{|w_a|^2}.
\tag{7.14}
\]
Here
\[
(g_1,\ldots,g_8)
=\left(\frac12,\frac12,\frac12,\frac12,
       \frac12,\frac12,1,\frac12\right),
\tag{7.15}
\]
so the actual limiting symbol determinant is
\[
\boxed{
\det[g_1C_1\ \cdots\ g_8C_8]=-\frac3{1280}.
}
\tag{7.16}
\]
It is still uniformly nonzero.  Thus the principal child endpoint map
controls the **full trace-free pointwise gradient**, not only its
symmetric part.

Even more explicitly,

\[
\sum_{a=1}^8\lambda_a C_a
=
\operatorname{diag}\left(-1,-\frac54,\frac94\right),
\tag{7.17}
\]

where

\[
(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_8)
=
\left(-2,-\frac52,1,1,\frac54,\frac54,0,0\right).
\tag{7.18}
\]

The nonzero scalar in the homochiral limit (6.3)--(6.8) is absorbed into
the complex product of the common carrier amplitude and the independently
controlled partner amplitude.

Choose every desired child coefficient in sine phase.  Then

\[
u_{\rm child}(x)
=\sum_a\lambda_ac_a\sin(q_a\cdot x)
\tag{7.19}
\]

satisfies

\[
u_{\rm child}(0)=0,\qquad
\nabla u_{\rm child}(0)=S.
\tag{7.20}
\]

There is no hidden velocity-value defect at principal order.  More
generally, the imaginary parts of the eight complex beat products control
the eight gradient coordinates, while their real parts control cosine
values.  Since

\[
\operatorname{span}\{c_a:1\le a\le8\}=\mathbb R^3,
\tag{7.21}
\]

the combined principal value-plus-gradient jet has rank \(3+8=11\).

The repeated vectors \(w_a=(1,1,0)\) do not represent duplicate Fourier
degrees of freedom.  They use one common parent mode and independently
controlled sideband partners \(l_a\).  At nonzero parent amplitude, the
eight partner amplitudes give the eight independent beat products.

## 8. One fast direction: an exact all-chain \(K\)-null structure

### Preferred three-sideband shear

Homochirality is not needed if every partner shares one fast direction.
Take

\[
w=e_3,\qquad k=Kw,\qquad a=e_1,
\]

and reuse the three low directions (1.1):

\[
r_j=q_j-Kw,\qquad j=1,2,3.
\]

Let \(b_j\in r_j^\perp\) be an independently controlled partner
polarization.  Since

\[
a\cdot r_j=a\cdot q_j,\qquad
b_j\cdot k=b_j\cdot q_j,
\]

the exact matched child map is

\[
L_{q_j,K}b_j
=
\mathbb P_{q_j}
\left(
(a\cdot q_j)b_j+(b_j\cdot q_j)a
\right).
\tag{8.A}
\]

This is a two-dimensional map \(r_j^\perp\to q_j^\perp\).

#### Proposition 8.1: exact finite-\(K\) rank two

Suppose

\[
a\cdot q\ne0,\qquad w\cdot q\ne0.
\tag{8.B}
\]

Then \(L_{q,K}\) is invertible unless

\[
2K(w\cdot q)=|q|^2.
\tag{8.C}
\]

Indeed, if the raw vector in (8.A) equals \(\lambda q\), dotting with
\(w=e_3\), using \(b\cdot(q-Kw)=0\), gives

\[
\lambda(w\cdot q)
=\frac{a\cdot q}{K}(b\cdot q).
\]

Dotting the same raw vector with \(q\) gives

\[
2(a\cdot q)(b\cdot q)=\lambda|q|^2.
\]

If \(b\cdot q=0\), then \(\lambda=0\) and (8.A) forces \(b=0\).
Otherwise the two identities give (8.C).

For the three vectors (1.1), \(q_xq_z\ne0\), and the exceptional values
are

\[
\frac{3721}{40},\qquad
\frac{61}{9},\qquad
\frac32.
\]

None is an integer.  Thus the three exact child maps have rank two for
every integer \(K>20\).

To see the uniform limiting chart, parameterize \(r^\perp\) by
\(\beta\in w^\perp\):

\[
b_K(\beta)
=
\beta+\frac{\beta\cdot q}{K-q_z}w.
\tag{8.D}
\]

Then \(b_K(\beta)=\beta+O(K^{-1})\), and

\[
L_{q,K}b_K(\beta)
\longrightarrow
L_{q,\infty}\beta
:=
\mathbb P_q\left(
(a\cdot q)\beta+(\beta\cdot q)a
\right).
\tag{8.E}
\]

The same kernel proof with \(\beta\cdot w=0\) shows that
\(L_{q,\infty}:w^\perp\to q^\perp\) is invertible.  For the basis
\(\beta=e_1,e_2\), the six symmetric-gradient columns associated with
the three \(q_j\)'s have exact rank five.  Their first five columns have
determinant

\[
\frac{1\,337\,394\,240\,000}{3721}

### 8.2 Exact variable-phase audit

Write a physical high field as

\[
w^K(t,x)=W(t,x,K\phi(t,x)),
\qquad
\int_{\mathbb T}W(t,x,\vartheta)\,d\vartheta=0,
\]

where \(\vartheta\) denotes the independent periodic angle.  Define

\[
{\cal D}_i=\partial_{x_i}+K\xi_i\partial_\vartheta,
\qquad \xi=\nabla\phi.
\]

Exact physical incompressibility is

\[
\operatorname{div}_xW
+K\xi\cdot\partial_\vartheta W=0.
\tag{8.K}
\]

Because \(W\) has zero angle mean, integrating (8.K) in \(\vartheta\)
gives

\[
K\xi\cdot W
=-\partial_\vartheta^{-1}\operatorname{div}_xW.
\tag{8.L}
\]

Hence the variable-phase high--high identity is still exact:

\[
W\cdot{\cal D}W
=
W\cdot\nabla_xW
-
\big(\partial_\vartheta^{-1}\operatorname{div}_xW\big)
\partial_\vartheta W.
\tag{8.M}
\]

No Hessian of \(\phi\) and no positive power of \(K\) occurs in this
convective term.  The phase gradient evolves by

\[
(\partial_t+U_{\rm low}\cdot\nabla)\xi
=-(\nabla U_{\rm low})^T\xi,
\tag{8.N}
\]

so deformation of the common material covector costs only one slow
gradient.

The Hessian of \(\phi\) first appears in second-order operators.  Direct
calculation gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
{\cal D}_i{\cal D}_iW
={}&\Delta_xW
+2K\xi\cdot\nabla_x\partial_\vartheta W
+K(\Delta\phi)\partial_\vartheta W\\
&+K^2|\xi|^2\partial_\vartheta^2W.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.O}
\]

After multiplication by normalized viscosity \(\varepsilon\), the last
term is the retained heat with coefficient
\(\theta=\varepsilon K^2\).  On the order-\(M\) band, the mixed and
Hessian terms cost at most

\[
C\frac{\theta}{K}
\left(M^2+M\|\Delta\phi\|\right),
\tag{8.P}
\]

up to the relevant Gevrey seminorms.  They contain no naked \(K\).

Pressure is the one part not settled by the algebraic cancellation.  On
a nonzero charge, its elliptic operator has principal part

\[
-K^2|\xi|^2\partial_\vartheta^2
\]

and lower terms exactly of the form displayed in (8.O).  The divergence
of the \(K\)-free nonlinear term (8.M) can contain one factor \(K\), but
the high-charge elliptic inverse contributes \(K^{-2}\), and the final
pressure gradient contributes at most one \(K\).  Symbolically this is
order zero, not order \(K\).  Turning that count into a uniform
Gevrey-2 estimate requires

\[
|\xi|\ge c>0
\]

and a no-repeated-loss material Leray/pressure parametrix.  That estimate
is **not** proved here.  The charge-zero pressure is the ordinary slow
elliptic problem and must be retained in \(U_{\rm low}\).  Although the
Euclidean scalar Laplacian in (8.O) displays only
\(\Delta\phi\), the full Hessian \(\nabla^2\phi=\nabla\xi\) enters
commutators and derivatives of the variable-coefficient pressure
parametrix; its Gevrey bounds are part of the same missing estimate.

Viscosity produces the favorable principal heat
\(\varepsilon K^2h^2|\xi|^2\).  Mixed fast--slow derivatives cost at most

\[
C\varepsilon KM^2
=C\theta\frac{M^2}{K},
\qquad \theta=\varepsilon K^2,
\tag{8.16}
\]

and retained backward heat costs \(\exp(C\theta M^2)\).  Both are tame in
the polynomial ledger.

### 8.3 A Gevrey-2 majorant

The worst algebraic factor at interaction order \(r\) in (8.9) is
\(Cr^2\).  Suppose lower-order coefficients obey

\[

\tag{8.17}
\]

The normalized quadratic convolution is bounded by

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{r^2}{(r!)^2}
\sum_{p=1}^{r-1}(p!)^2((r-p)!)^2
&=
r^2\sum_{p=1}^{r-1}\binom rp^{-2}\\
&\le C.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.18}
\]

The last bound is uniform in \(r\); separating the two endpoint terms
from the interior gives an elementary proof.  A quadratic majorant
induction therefore closes

\[
\boxed{a_r\le C_0C_1^r(r!)^2}
\tag{8.19}
\]

for a forward hierarchy whose slow flow, envelopes, and sources have
Gevrey order at most two.

## 9. What the repair proves, and the remaining theorem

The preferred single-parent construction resolves the two phase-level
obstructions found in the original proposal.

* The three exact partner maps are rank two for every sufficiently large
  integer \(K\), and their six symmetric columns have rank five.
* The exact high--high identity (8.J) removes \(K\) on the **complete**
  generated charge lattice, not only at the initial shell.
* Transport by the full generated low child is absorbed into one material
  phase, rather than frozen at the affine linearization.
* The resulting coefficient recursion has a Gevrey-2 factorial majorant
  compatible with \(K_j=j^A,\ A>4\).

The eight-sideband two-direction chart remains useful if a full
\(\mathfrak{sl}(3)\) endpoint submersion is essential, but its
off-shell-chain problem is genuine.  The adversarial audit
[`2026-07-29-beltrami-sideband-star-adversarial-audit.md`](2026-07-29-beltrami-sideband-star-adversarial-audit.md)
exhibits it explicitly.  With
\[
k_1=K(1,1,0),\qquad
k_2=K(1,-1,0),\qquad
l_1=(-K-1,-K,0),
\]
one first unmatched interaction is \(O(1)\), while its subsequent
interaction with \(k_1\) has an \(O(K)\) projected coefficient.  Thus
first-annulus transparency alone cannot prove a tame two-direction
semigroup.

The revised preferred theorem is:

> **Single-parent forward material-phase theorem.**  Use the three
> original low directions with the common parent
> \(k=Ke_3,\ a=e_1\), preferably in three sequential partner pulses.
> Construct the full low Lagrangian flow and the finite charged hierarchy
> through \(M_j\asymp j^2/\log j\) so that:
>
> 1. the low eikonal flow stays Gevrey-2 and nondegenerate,
>    \(|\nabla\phi|\ge c>0\);
> 2. the exact rank-five child endpoint map survives the sequential
>    nonlinear feedback;
> 3. the material high-charge pressure solver has a no-repeated-loss
>    Gevrey-2 bound;
> 4. the charged hierarchy obeys \(C^m(m!)^2\) uniformly through \(M_j\);
> 5. the retained heat inverse costs only
>    \(\exp(C\theta_jM_j^2)=1+o(1)\);
> 6. every nonzero terminal charge is exported to the annular wake; and
> 7. localization, material-coordinate, and seam remainders are
>    \(e^{-cj^2}\).

The exact forward formulation proves there are no static adjoint
compatibilities to solve.  What remains is no longer a mysterious
