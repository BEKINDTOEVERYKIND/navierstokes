## 0. Claim boundary

C179 supplies an exact unforced 2D3C background, while C181 proves that a
single static vertical shear is bounded on the fixed C159 projective cone.
The missing case is an advected passive scalar interleaved with the planar
\(A_2\) deformation over many returns.  It cannot be replaced by an
endpoint Piola--Leray formula.

This note makes the surviving object exact.  For

\[
             U(x_h,z,t)=(v_h(x_h,t),\Theta(x_h,t)),       \tag{0.1}
\]

with inviscidly passive \(\Theta\), the horizontal Kelvin covector obeys

\[
       \boxed{D(t,a)^T p(t)=p_0-mt\,g_0},\qquad
       g_0=\nabla_a\Theta_0(a),                            \tag{0.2}
\]

where \(D=\nabla_aX\) is the planar deformation and \(m\) is the conserved
vertical charge.  At the exact finite-frequency PDE level, vertical
transport is removed by the unit-modulus phase

\[
                         e^{-imt\Theta_0(a)}.              \tag{0.3}
\]

All possible amplitude gain is left in a time-ordered
polarization/pressure connection.  The connection has an exact smooth
Floquet frame only when two return-resonance identities hold.  Under that
resonance, an \(O(q^{-1/2})\) per-period connection perturbation over
\(O(\log q)\) returns contributes only \(1+o(1)\), not \(\sqrt q\).

Neither C180 nor PPRG is thereby closed.  C180's reservoir is preloaded
throughout the long C159 window, and its persistence on one common periodic
trajectory/covector frame has not been proved.  A general PPRG scalar need
not satisfy the return resonance and can create a linearly or quadratically
drifting covector in return coordinates.  The remaining named obstruction
is therefore **common-Floquet-frame closure (CFFC)**: either construct a
return-coherent frame and certify its full connection, or control the
nonperiodic time-ordered connection generated by its explicit return
defects.  No endpoint deformation formula removes this obligation.

## 1. Exact triangular Lagrangian geometry

Use coordinates \(x=(x_h,z)\), with \(x_h\in\mathbb R^2\).  Along a planar
trajectory

\[
       \dot X(t,a)=v_h(t,X(t,a)),\qquad D(t,a)=\nabla_aX(t,a),
\]

write

\[
 M=\nabla_hv_h(t,X),\qquad g=\nabla_h\Theta(t,X),\qquad
 A=\nabla U=\begin{pmatrix}M&0\\g^T&0\end{pmatrix}.       \tag{1.1}
\]

Planar incompressibility gives \(\operatorname{tr}M=0\),
\(D'=MD\), and \(\det D=1\).  Inviscid passivity gives

\[
 \Theta(t,X(t,a))=\Theta_0(a),\qquad
                   g(t,X(t,a))=D(t,a)^{-T}g_0.           \tag{1.2}
\]

Let a Kelvin covector be \(k=(p,m)\).  Since \(k'=-A^Tk\),

\[
             p'=-M^Tp-mg,\qquad m'=0.                    \tag{1.3}
\]

Multiplication by \(D^T\) and use of (1.2) yield

\[
 {d\over dt}(D^Tp)=D^TM^Tp+D^Tp'=-mD^Tg=-mg_0,          \tag{1.4}
\]

which proves (0.2).  Equivalently,

\[
              p(t)=D(t)^{-T}(p_0-mt\,g_0).               \tag{1.5}
\]

This is an endpoint formula for the **covector only**.  It is not an
endpoint formula for the Kelvin velocity amplitude.

## 2. Exact vertical-Fourier gauge

Take one vertical Fourier mode of a linearized Euler perturbation,
\(V=(u,w)e^{imz}\), with pressure \(P e^{imz}\).  Before any geometric-optics
limit, its exact equations are

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &(\partial_t+v_h\cdot\nabla_h+im\Theta)V
       +A V+(\nabla_hP,imP)=0,\\
 &\nabla_h\cdot u+imw=0.                                 \tag{2.1}
 \end{aligned}
\]

In planar labels define \(B=(b,\eta)\) and \(\Pi\) by

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 V(t,X(t,a))&=e^{-imt\Theta_0(a)}B(t,a),\\
 P(t,X(t,a))&=e^{-imt\Theta_0(a)}\Pi(t,a).
 \end{aligned}                                         \tag{2.2}
\]

Then (2.1) is exactly

\[
 \boxed{
 \partial_t B+A B+
 \begin{pmatrix}
 D^{-T}(\nabla_a-imt\,g_0)\Pi\\ im\Pi
 \end{pmatrix}=0,}                                     \tag{2.3}
\]

with the exact Piola-form constraint

\[
 \boxed{
 \nabla_a\cdot(D^{-1}b)-imt\,g_0\cdot D^{-1}b
       +im\eta=0.}                                      \tag{2.4}
\]

Thus the first term is explicitly \(\operatorname{div}_a(D^{-1}b)\), not
the componentwise expression \(D^{-T}:\nabla_ab\) with derivatives of
\(D\) discarded.  The Piola identity is legitimate because \(\det D=1\).
The
pullback by \(X\) is volume preserving and the factor (0.3) has modulus
one.  Hence the transport-plus-phase part alone is an \(L^r\) isometry for
every \(1\le r\le\infty\).  It may shift derivatives and frequencies, but
it supplies no pointwise amplitude factor.

For a label phase with horizontal covector \(p_0\), the covariant
derivative in (2.3)--(2.4) reads \(p_0-mt g_0\), and pushforward by
\(D^{-T}\) gives (1.5).  This observation identifies the principal
covector; it does not turn a label plane wave into an exact global Fourier
mode when \(D\) depends on \(a\), and no finite-frequency estimate is
claimed from it.

## 3. The residual Kelvin polarization connection

For an amplitude \(a=(u,w)\in k^\perp\), set

\[
       \sigma=k\cdot Aa=p\cdot Mu+m\,g\cdot u,
       \qquad r^2=|k|^2.                                \tag{3.1}
\]

The actual incompressible Euler Kelvin system is

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 k'&=-A^Tk,\\
 a'&=K(t)a,qquad
 K(t)=-A+2{k(k^TA)\over r^2},\\
 k\cdot a&=0.
 \end{aligned}}                                        \tag{3.2}
\]

In components,

\[
       u'=-Mu+2p{\sigma\over r^2},\qquad
       w'=-g\cdot u+2m{\sigma\over r^2}.                \tag{3.3}
\]

The constraint is preserved because

\[
       (k\cdot a)'=-\sigma-\sigma+2\sigma=0.             \tag{3.4}
\]

Choose any smooth orthonormal frame \(E(t)=(e_1,e_2)\) of \(k(t)^\perp\)
and write \(a=Ez\).  The exact two-polarization equation is

\[
             z'=B(t)z,\qquad
             B=E^TKE-E^TE'.                             \tag{3.5}
\]

This is the residual polarization/pressure connection.  It is a
time-ordered exponential, not a scalar passive multiplier.  Its trace has
the exact frame-independent identity

\[
       \operatorname{tr}B={k^TAk\over |k|^2}
          =-{d\over dt}\log|k|,                          \tag{3.6}
\]

so the oriented area multiplier between orthonormal fibers is

\[
                  \det\Phi(t,0)={|k(0)|\over|k(t)|}.     \tag{3.7}
\]

In particular a periodic covector has unit determinant monodromy.  This
does not bound its largest singular value.

The false endpoint-Cauchy shortcut can be seen directly.  If
\(\omega=k\times a\) and \(\Omega=\operatorname{curl}U\), then (3.2) gives

\[
                \boxed{\omega'=A\omega+(\Omega\cdot k)a.} \tag{3.8}
\]

The second term is generally nonzero.  Cauchy's deformation formula for a
material vorticity vector therefore does not propagate this Kelvin
velocity perturbation.  C181's nonsymmetric vertical shear is an explicit
closed-form instance where \(P_{F^{-T}k}F^{-T}a\) differs from (3.2).

## 4. Exact return resonance

Assume the marked planar trajectory and the planar base close/repeat after
\(T\).  In particular \(M(t+T)=M(t)\), and its fundamental matrix obeys

\[
                         D(t+T)=D(t)D_T.                 \tag{4.0}
\]

Put

\[
                 D_T=D(T),\qquad L=D_T^T.                \tag{4.1}
\]

The fundamental-matrix composition law and (1.2), (1.5) give at the
\(n\)-th return

\[
 \boxed{
       g_n=L^{-n}g_0,\qquad
       p_n=L^{-n}(p_0-mnTg_0).}                          \tag{4.2}
\]

The full coefficient \((A,k)\), and hence a smooth polarization connection,
is \(T\)-periodic precisely when the relevant orbit data satisfy

\[
             \boxed{Lg_0=g_0,\qquad
                    Lp_0=p_0-mTg_0.}                    \tag{4.3}
\]

The first identity makes the passive gradient return; the second is the
affine covector resonance.  Under (4.3), (1.2) and (1.5) are periodic for
all intermediate phases, not only at the endpoint.

The closed planar-orbit return is commonly unipotent.  If
\(L=I+N\), \(N^2=0\), then (4.2) becomes the exact drift ledger

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 g_n&=g_0-nNg_0,\\
 p_n&=p_0-n(Np_0+mTg_0)+mn^2T\,Ng_0.
 \end{aligned}}                                        \tag{4.4}
\]

Thus \(Ng_0=0\) and \(Np_0=-mTg_0\) give exact return coherence.  A defect
in the affine resonance produces linear drift; a noninvariant passive
gradient produces quadratic drift.  This is why an arbitrary advected
PPRG palette does not automatically share the C159 Floquet frame.

### 4.1 Exact specialization to the C152 zero loop

Let \(\gamma_f=\nabla f(X_0)\), let \(U_0=v_h(X_0)\), and denote the
period derivative of the neighboring planar level loops by \(T'(0)\).
C152's exact planar return is

\[
 D_T=I+u_h\otimes\gamma_f,
 \qquad u_h=-T'(0)U_0,
 \qquad u_h\cdot\gamma_f=0.                              \tag{4.5}
\]

Consequently

\[
                         L=I+\gamma_f\otimes u_h.         \tag{4.6}
\]

Take a stationary inviscid scalar \(\Theta=H(f)\), put \(H(0)=0\), and
write \(c=H'(0)\) for its scalar derivative in the unit vertical
direction.  Then \(g_0=c\gamma_f\), and the first return identity in
(4.3) is automatic.  The second reduces to the single affine condition

\[
                         \boxed{u_h\cdot p_0=-mTc.}       \tag{4.7}
\]

This is not merely a planar artifact.  Differentiating the vertical
displacement \(T(E)H(E)\) at \(E=0\) gives \(Tc\).  Hence the full
three-dimensional deformation return is

\[
 F_T=I+(u_h+Tc\,n)\otimes\gamma_f,                       \tag{4.8}
\]

and

\[
 F_T^T(p_0+mn)=p_0+mn
       +(u_h\cdot p_0+mTc)\gamma_f.                      \tag{4.9}
\]

Thus (4.7) is exactly the full periodic-covector plane condition.  For
the original C152 scalar \(\Theta=-\sqrt2 fN\), unit vertical coordinate
gives \(c=-\sqrt6\), so \(Tc\,n=-\sqrt2TN\), agreeing with C152's sign.
Stationary \(H(f)\) reservoirs therefore admit an exact common return
frame after retuning \(p_0\).  This sharpens CFFC: the unresolved part is
the broad nonstationary PPRG palette, or the full connection after such a
retuning, not existence of a returning frame for every passive scalar.

## 5. What a common Floquet frame would settle

On the fixed C159 projective tube, \(k\) stays away from zero and from the
vertical frame singularity.  If (4.3) holds, one may take the explicit
periodic frame

\[
 e_1={|k|\over|p|}\left(n-{m\over|k|^2}k\right),
 \qquad e_2={k\times n\over|p|}.                         \tag{5.1}
\]

Both vectors have unit norm, they are orthogonal, and
\(e_1\times e_2=k/|k|\); this fixes the orientation.  Then \(B(t)\) in
(3.5) is periodic and ordinary two-dimensional Floquet
theory applies.  The passive phase acquired over a return is the scalar
\(e^{-imT\Theta_0(a)}\), so it changes no Floquet modulus.

There is a useful conditional long-window theorem.  Let \(B_0\) be the
periodic C159 connection, with simple hyperbolic monodromy eigenvalues
\(\rho_0>1\) and \(\rho_0^{-1}\).  Let \(B_q\) be another periodic
connection in a common smooth frame and suppose

\[
          \int_0^T\|B_q(t)-B_0(t)\|\,dt\le\varepsilon_q. \tag{5.2}
\]

Variation of constants gives an \(O(\varepsilon_q)\) perturbation of the
one-period monodromy.  Simplicity of \(\rho_0\) then gives, uniformly for
all integers \(N\ge0\) and all sufficiently small \(\varepsilon_q\),

\[
             \boxed{
\|\Phi_q(NT,0)\|
      \le C\rho_0^N\exp(CN\varepsilon_q).}              \tag{5.3}
\]

Here \(C\) depends on the fixed reference connection and its (possibly
nonnormal) eigenvector condition number, but not on \(q\) or \(N\).
Indeed, the hyperbolic \(2\times2\) monodromy remains diagonalizable with
uniformly bounded spectral projectors, and its dominant eigenvalue is at
most \(\rho_0\exp(C\varepsilon_q)\).  This proves (5.3) even when the
one-period monodromy is not normal.

Consequently, if \(\varepsilon_q=O(q^{-1/2})\) and
\(N=O(\log q)\), the extra factor in (5.3) is \(1+o(1)\), and cannot be
the missing \(\sqrt q\).

The hypotheses of this theorem are load bearing.  C180's same-shell
coefficient ledger gives only an \(O(q^{-1/2})\) **spatial coefficient
ledger** in a fixed normalized \(C^2\) norm.  It does not imply
\(\varepsilon_q=O(q^{-1/2})\) in (5.2), nor does it give a common closed
trajectory, a return-periodic covector, or one smooth frame.  Its reservoir
also cannot be terminally switched on: it is preloaded throughout the
\(O(\log q)\) C159 gain window.  Therefore (5.3) does **not** close the
autonomous C180 branch.

For PPRG, the 2D3C reduction is exact, but its \(qK\)-frequency scalar can
have large gradient and nonzero defects in (4.3).  It lies outside the
small periodic perturbation theorem unless those defects and the full
connection are separately controlled.  Such nonperiodic return drift is
not a new geometry; it is the remaining time-ordered mechanism on the
existing C159/C176/C179 cell.

## 6. The named obstruction

The exact next obligation is CFFC:

> **Common-Floquet-frame closure.**  On the C159 projective input tube and
> through the required \(O(\log q)\) preloaded returns, either construct a
> smooth return-coherent frame satisfying (4.3) and bound its full
> polarization connection (3.5), or exploit and control the explicit
> return defects (4.4) to obtain \(\sqrt q\) physical focusing while paying
> reverse edges, finite-frequency leakage, heat, localization, retained
> wake, and the C125/RIGM/BAFL ledgers.

If the first alternative has connection error \(O(q^{-1/2})\), (5.3)
rules out the missing gain.  The second alternative remains open.  An
instantaneous edge determinant, the passive-scalar maximum principle, or
an endpoint deformation matrix does not settle it.

## 7. Verification boundary

The dependency-free checker verifies with exact rational arithmetic the
passive-gradient and covector reduction, the covariant gauge frequency and
Piola constraint on label plane waves, preservation of the Kelvin
constraint, the vorticity correction (3.8), the connection trace/area
identity, the return formulas (4.2)--(4.4), and the affine resonance.  It
also checks the stationary-scalar C152 specialization (4.5)--(4.9) and the
\(q^{-1/2}\log q\) Floquet ledger.  It does not prove the
standard finite-dimensional Floquet perturbation theorem, construct a
C180/PPRG common frame, promote the Kelvin system to a localized
finite-frequency estimate, or verify any one-cell stage obligation.
