# C178: compact heat preparation is approximate; an analytic buffer is exact

**Date:** 2026-08-13

**Status:** exact fixed-profile compact heat-range obstruction, exact

## 0. Claim boundary

C174 and C176 use prescribed rigid-collar ramps.  Their residuals fit the
scalar BAFL powers, but an unforced stage must absorb those residuals into
the initial datum and the subsequent Navier--Stokes correction rather than
declare an external force.  This note decides the pure heat part of that
problem on the existing \(A_2\) cell.  The no-go theorem concerns one
fixed spatial profile multiplied by a nonnegative scalar ramp.  The local
approximation also covers a smooth time-dependent source whose
instantaneous supports lie in one common compact swept tube.  Neither
statement classifies exact cancellation for a signed or moving
multi-profile bundle.

There are four different statements, and conflating them hides the
parabolic obstruction.

1. **Exact fixed-profile terminal preparation fails.**  A nonzero compact
   collar profile applied by a smooth monotone scalar ramp at positive time
   cannot be canceled exactly at the terminal time by any \(L^2\) initial
   datum for the heat equation.  The required datum contains a backward
   Gaussian multiplier.  If it were in \(L^2\), the compact collar profile
   would be a positive-time heat image and hence analytic, forcing it to
   vanish.
2. **Exact analytic-buffer preparation succeeds.**  If a raw compact
   divergence-free collar \(h\) is replaced by the heat-buffered profile
   \(g=e^{\sigma\Delta}h\), \(\sigma\ge\nu T\), then an explicit
   divergence-free \(L^2\) initial datum cancels its pure-heat ramp response
   exactly and costs no more than \(\|h\|_2\).  For \(\sigma>\nu T\), both
   fields have analytic global tails.  With \(\sigma=2\nu T\) and
   \(\nu T/R^2=O(q^{-1})\), they differ from the raw affine core only by
   \(e^{-cq}\), up to fixed derivative polynomials, on a buffered inner
   core.
3. **Arbitrarily accurate compact preparation succeeds.**  Truncating the
   backward exponential by a polynomial in \(-\Delta\) produces a smooth,
   compactly supported, exactly divergence-free initial correction on the
   same support as the collar.  Its terminal heat/Duhamel response converges
   to zero in \(L^2\).  No spatial cutoff is needed for this approximation.
4. **Compactness is instantaneous only.**  The homogeneous component
   generated by every nonzero prepared datum has global analytic tails for
   every positive time.  Thus an unforced viscous realization cannot retain
   literal moving compact support for that component; its tails must enter
   the pressure/heat channel of BAFL.

For a Gevrey-2 collar and heat number

\[
             \alpha={\nu T\over R^2}\lesssim q^{-1},      \tag{0.1}
\]

the degree-one compact preparation already makes the terminal multiplier

\[
 O\!\left(q^{-2}(\log n)^8+n^{-A}\right)                 \tag{0.2}
\]

after taking dimensionless Fourier cutoff
\(L=(A_0\log n)^2\), with \(A=c_0A_0\).  Since \(q=n^8\), this is more
than enough to push
the **terminal heat error** below the \(n^{-28}\) C125 seed on C176's
collar ledger.

That does not yet prove C125.  With
\(R=J\ell/q\), \(J=O(\log h)\), the total prepared initial norm has the
raw parent-normalized scale

\[
 S_{\rm prep}\lesssim
       \Lambda J^{7/2}q^{-5/2}
       =\Lambda J^{7/2}n^{-20},                            \tag{0.3}
\]

where \(\Lambda=\lambda\ell/a\).  Relative to
\(\varepsilon=n^{-28}\), this is

\[
                  {S_{\rm prep}\over\varepsilon}
       \lesssim \Lambda J^{7/2}n^8.                       \tag{0.4}
\]

For an order-one C159 rate this diverges.  Hence terminal heat cancellation
alone does not protect the microseed: the prepared trajectory must be dark
to the C125 left growing functional to relative order
\(O(n^{-8}/(\Lambda J^{7/2}))\), or its coupling must be included in the
intended unforced dynamics.  This is the physical-space counterpart of
C175's graph--Melnikov gate and the autonomous reservoir tested in C177.

There is one favorable subbranch.  On C142's tuned viscous selector,

\[
 \Lambda\asymp{\mu q^3\over\log n},\qquad
 \mu=\nu((n-1)!)^{-2},                                    \tag{0.5}
\]

(0.4) is

\[
 O\!\left(\mu n^{32}{J^{7/2}\over\log n}\right)=o(1),   \tag{0.6}
\]

and (0.1) holds with \(\alpha=O(q^{-1})\).  Thus on that
specific rate schedule both the compact approximate preparation and the
exact analytic-buffer preparation are smaller than the C125 seed after all
fixed polynomial factors.  This is a genuine positive scalar opening, not
an unforced stage theorem: the analytic buffer perturbs the exact affine
core, and the C142 material-envelope obstruction, the A2 linearized
propagator, nonlinear interactions, pressure/tails, and the retained wake
remain to be controlled.

## 1. The exact heat preparation equation

Let \(g\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^3;\mathbb R^3)\) be nonzero and
divergence free.  In the fixed-profile model it represents one frozen
collar difference.  Let \(\vartheta\in C^\infty([0,T])\) be monotone, with

\[
 \vartheta'(s)\ge0,\qquad
 \int_0^T\vartheta'(s)\,ds=1.                             \tag{1.1}
\]

Consider the correction equation

\[
 \partial_tw-\nu\Delta w=-\vartheta'(t)g,qquad w(0)=z_0. \tag{1.2}
\]

The minus sign means that \(w\) cancels the residual of the prescribed
ramp.  Calling (1.2) a forced correction does not introduce an external
force into the target solution: if the approximate field has residual
\(+\vartheta'g\), their sum has no such residual.  The full unforced
Navier--Stokes correction additionally contains the linearized and
quadratic terms omitted from (1.2).

Writing \(x=|\xi|^2\), Duhamel's formula gives

\[
 \widehat w(T,\xi)=e^{-\nu Tx}\widehat z_0(\xi)
 -\left\{\int_0^T\vartheta'(s)e^{-\nu(T-s)x}\,ds\right\}
    \widehat g(\xi).                                      \tag{1.3}
\]

Therefore exact terminal cancellation would require

\[
 \boxed{
 \widehat z_0(\xi)=a(x)\widehat g(\xi),\qquad
 a(x)=\int_0^T\vartheta'(s)e^{\nu sx}\,ds.}              \tag{1.4}
\]

This is the exact backward-heat cost.  Since \(a(x)\ge1\), preparation
cannot reduce the \(L^2\) norm even in a spectrally truncated model.

## 2. Exact obstruction to an \(L^2\) terminal null preparation

Because the ramp is smooth and nonconstant, there are
\(s_0>0\) and \(\gamma>0\) such that

\[
                 \int_{s_0}^T\vartheta'(s)\,ds=\gamma.    \tag{2.1}
\]

Consequently

\[
                         a(x)\ge\gamma e^{\nu s_0x}.       \tag{2.2}
\]

If the datum in (1.4) belonged to \(L^2\), then

\[
                 e^{\nu s_0|\xi|^2}\widehat g(\xi)
                    \in L^2_\xi.                          \tag{2.3}
\]

Define \(h\in L^2\) by
\(\widehat h=e^{\nu s_0|\xi|^2}\widehat g\).  Equation (2.3) says

\[
                              g=e^{\nu s_0\Delta}h.        \tag{2.4}
\]

A positive-time heat image of an \(L^2\) field is real analytic.  Since
\(g\) vanishes on the nonempty exterior of its compact support, analytic
unique continuation gives \(g\equiv0\), a contradiction.  Hence:

> **Exact fixed-profile compact-ramp range obstruction.**  For every
> nonzero compact profile \(g\) and every nontrivial smooth monotone
> positive-time scalar ramp, no \(L^2\) initial datum makes (1.2) vanish
> exactly at time \(T\).

The conclusion is componentwise and therefore applies to divergence-free
vector fields.  The identical proof holds on a torus when the collar is
supported in a proper chart: a positive-time periodic heat image is real
analytic, and vanishing on the complementary open set forces zero.  It is
not a no-go for a signed collection of different profiles, or a genuinely
time-dependent moving profile, whose backward Gaussian tails cancel
frequency by frequency.  Such cancellation is an exact
infinite-dimensional compatibility condition not classified here.

There is a second immediate compactness obstruction.  For any nonzero
\(z_0\in L^2\), the homogeneous field
\(e^{\nu t\Delta}z_0\) is analytic and noncompactly supported for every
\(t>0\).  Thus literal compact localization cannot be preserved
dynamically by the homogeneous part of a nonzero viscous preparation.
Gaussian/analytic tails, rather than compact support, are the correct state
class after the cell starts.  A continuously applied nonanalytic source
need not make the entire forced solution analytic uniformly up to its
current time; no such stronger statement is used here.

### 2.1 Exact controllability after an analytic heat buffer

The range obstruction identifies its own sharp repair.  Let
\(h\in L^2(\mathbb R^3;\mathbb R^3)\) be divergence free, choose
\(\sigma\ge\nu T\), and replace the ramp profile by

\[
                         g=e^{\sigma\Delta}h.               \tag{2.5}
\]

Define

\[
 \boxed{
 z_0^{\rm an}=\int_0^T\vartheta'(s)
                  e^{(\sigma-\nu s)\Delta}h\,ds.}          \tag{2.6}
\]

Every heat factor in (2.6) is forward because
\(\sigma-\nu s\ge0\).  The heat semigroup commutes with divergence and is
an \(L^2\) contraction, so

\[
 \nabla\cdot g=\nabla\cdot z_0^{\rm an}=0,
 \qquad
 \|g\|_2,\ \|z_0^{\rm an}\|_2\le\|h\|_2.                \tag{2.7}
\]

The semigroup law gives the exact terminal identity

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 e^{\nu T\Delta}z_0^{\rm an}
 &=\int_0^T\vartheta'(s)
     e^{(\sigma+\nu(T-s))\Delta}h\,ds\\
 &=\int_0^T\vartheta'(s)e^{\nu(T-s)\Delta}g\,ds.          \tag{2.8}
 \end{aligned}
\]

Thus the solution of (1.2) with \(z_0=z_0^{\rm an}\) satisfies
\(w(T)=0\) **exactly**.  This is a positive terminal-controllability lemma
for the pure heat block.  In the intended combined field the source in
(1.2) is the negative of the prescribed approximate-field residual; this
lemma does not cancel any of the omitted linearized or nonlinear residuals.

It necessarily gives up compact support.  If \(h\ne0\) is compactly
supported and \(\sigma>\nu T\), both (2.5) and every integrand in (2.6)
are analytic with global heat tails.  Moreover, no nonzero analytic field
can agree exactly with a prescribed affine polynomial on an open core and
then localize elsewhere: unique continuation would make it that affine
field globally.  Exact analytic-buffer preparation therefore perturbs the
rigid affine core as well as its exterior.

That perturbation is exponentially localized when the buffer is shorter
than the collar scale.  Suppose a compact curl collar \(h\) equals the
affine field \(Sx\) on \(B_{2R}\), where \(\|S\|\le C\lambda\), and that
\(h\) is a fixed rescaled profile supported in \(B_{C_*R}\), with the
corresponding fixed rescaled seminorms.  Take \(\sigma=2\nu T\).  Standard
Gaussian-kernel estimates on \(B_R\), comparing with the heat-stationary
polynomial \(Sx\), give for each fixed derivative order \(m\)

\[
 \sup_{B_R}\left|
  \nabla^m(e^{\tau\Delta}h-Sx)\right|
 \le C_m\lambda R^{1-m}
       \alpha^{-C_m}e^{-c_m/\alpha},
 \quad \nu T\le\tau\le2\nu T,
 \quad \alpha={\nu T\over R^2}.                           \tag{2.9}
\]

The same bound holds for the average (2.6).  When
\(\alpha=O(q^{-1})\), every fixed derivative/core error is
\(O(e^{-cq})\) after absorbing polynomial factors.  This estimate requires
the displayed fixed-profile and full-buffer-annulus hypotheses.  It does
not prove that the slightly perturbed
Kelvin/A2 cocycle has the required C125 right/left trajectories; that
robustness remains part of the packet theorem.

## 3. Exact compact divergence-free approximate preparation

The range obstruction is exact but not quantitative: the range is dense.
Here that density has a particularly simple local construction.  Define
the ramp moments

\[
                         m_k=\int_0^Ts^k\vartheta'(s)\,ds  \tag{3.1}
\]

and, for \(M\ge0\), put

\[
 \boxed{
 z_0^{(M)}=
   \sum_{k=0}^M{\nu^km_k\over k!}(-\Delta)^kg.}           \tag{3.2}
\]

Every term is smooth, supported in \(\operatorname{supp}g\), and
divergence free.  Thus

\[
 z_0^{(M)}\in C_c^\infty,qquad
 \operatorname{supp}z_0^{(M)}\subseteq\operatorname{supp}g,qquad
 \nabla\cdot z_0^{(M)}=0.                                \tag{3.3}
\]

Substitution in (1.3) gives the exact error

\[
 \widehat w_M(T,\xi)=
 -\int_0^T\vartheta'(s)e^{-\nu T x}
       \sum_{k=M+1}^\infty{(\nu sx)^k\over k!}\,ds
       \widehat g(\xi).                                  \tag{3.4}
\]

The multiplier in absolute value is at most

\[
 \int_0^T\vartheta'(s)e^{-\nu(T-s)x}\,ds\le1.           \tag{3.5}
\]

For each fixed \(\xi\), it tends to zero as \(M\to\infty\).  Dominated
convergence against \(|\widehat g|^2\) proves

\[
                              \|w_M(T)\|_2\longrightarrow0. \tag{3.6}
\]

Thus arbitrary terminal accuracy is compatible with **initial** compact
support and exact incompressibility, even though exact cancellation and
positive-time compact support are impossible.  Formula (3.2) is local and
requires no spectral projection, so it does not introduce the global
band-limited tail that a sharp Fourier truncation would create.

The construction is not limited to a separated source.  For a smooth
divergence-free \(F(s,x)\) whose supports remain in one compact swept tube,
replace (3.1)--(3.2) by

\[
 G_k(x)=\int_0^Ts^kF(s,x)\,ds,
 \qquad
 z_0^{(M)}=\sum_{k=0}^M{\nu^k\over k!}(-\Delta)^kG_k.     \tag{3.7}
\]

Every \(G_k\), and hence \(z_0^{(M)}\), is divergence free and supported
in that swept tube.  Fourier substitution gives the Taylor truncation of
the exact formal datum
\(\int_0^Te^{\nu s|\xi|^2}\widehat F(s,\xi)ds\).  For each fixed \(s\),
the terminal Taylor-remainder multiplier tends pointwise to zero and is
bounded by one.  Strong dominated convergence in \(L^2_x\), followed by
Bochner dominated convergence in \(s\), proves terminal convergence for
\(F\in L^1([0,T];L^2)\).  The assumed smooth common-support source has
this property.  The clean quantitative constants below are stated for the
separated ramp, which is the exact C174 switching model on a frozen
overlap.  A moving profile must charge its uniform time-dependent
seminorms.

## 4. A quantitative Gevrey-2 version

Let the collar have spatial scale \(R\), and suppose its dimensionless
Fourier tail obeys

\[
 \left\|1_{\{R|\xi|>L\}}\widehat g\right\|_2
       \le C_0e^{-c_0\sqrt L}\|g\|_2.                    \tag{4.1}
\]

Compact Gevrey-2 cutoffs can satisfy (4.1).  Put

\[
                              \alpha={\nu T\over R^2}.     \tag{4.2}
\]

For \(R|\xi|\le L\), the Poisson-tail inequality

\[
 e^{-y}\sum_{k=M+1}^\infty{y^k\over k!}
       \le {y^{M+1}\over(M+1)!},\qquad y\ge0,             \tag{4.3}
\]

applied with \(y=\nu T|\xi|^2\le\alpha L^2\), together
with (3.5) on the complement, gives

\[
 \boxed{
 {\|w_M(T)\|_2\over\|g\|_2}
 \le { (\alpha L^2)^{M+1}\over(M+1)!}
       +C_0e^{-c_0\sqrt L}.}                              \tag{4.4}
\]

The initial-data cost is, for every fixed \(M\),

\[
 \|z_0^{(M)}\|_2
 \le\sum_{k=0}^M{(\nu T)^k\over k!}\|(-\Delta)^kg\|_2.
                                                               \tag{4.5}
\]

If the fixed rescaled collar profile has
\(\|(-\Delta)^kg\|_2\le C_kR^{-2k}\|g\|_2\), then

\[
                    \|z_0^{(M)}\|_2
       \le C_M(1+\alpha^M)\|g\|_2.                       \tag{4.6}
\]

In particular, small heat number makes a low-degree local preparation
comparable in norm to the original collar jump.  It does not make that
jump smaller.

## 5. The C176/C125 schedule

Use

\[
 q=n^8,\qquad b=n^{-2},\qquad h=n^{12},\qquad
 J\asymp\log h,qquad R={J\ell\over q}.                   \tag{5.1}
\]

One C176 enlarged-collar jump has parent-normalized \(L^2\) size

\[
 {\lambda R^{5/2}\over a\ell^{3/2}}
       =\Lambda J^{5/2}q^{-5/2}.                          \tag{5.2}
\]

Preparing \(J\) such jumps by a sum-of-norms argument gives (0.3).  The
corresponding total-window heat number is

\[
 \boxed{
 \alpha={\nu T\over R^2}
 = {\mu q^2\log h\over\Lambda J^2},\qquad
 \mu={\nu\over a\ell}=\nu((n-1)!)^{-2}.}                 \tag{5.3}
\]

This identity shows that C176's upper rate condition alone does not control
backward heat: an arbitrarily tiny \(\Lambda\) makes \(\alpha\) large.
The low-degree compact approximation with the logarithmic Fourier split,
and the analytic-buffer branch if it must preserve the affine core to
superpolynomial accuracy, have the additional heat gate

\[
                       \alpha(\log n)^4=O(1)              \tag{5.4}
\]

for the displayed estimates.  Exact analytic-buffer terminal cancellation
and its \(L^2\) contraction bound (2.7)--(2.8) hold for every \(\alpha\);
large \(\alpha\) simply destroys localization and affine-core fidelity.

The preparation energy is not a global schedule obstruction.  Its fraction
of the parent energy is bounded by

\[
                    S_{\rm prep}^2
       \lesssim\Lambda^2J^7n^{-40},                       \tag{5.4a}
\]

which is summable after multiplication by C127's factorial parent energy.
For order-one \(\Lambda\), however, the microseed energy is \(n^{-56}\),
so the preparation-to-seed energy ratio is
\(\Lambda^2J^7n^{16}\).  Energy summability therefore does not imply
C125 relative smallness.

Take \(M=1\) and \(L=(A_0\log n)^2\).  If
\(\alpha\le Cq^{-1}=Cn^{-8}\), (4.4) becomes

\[
 {\|w_1(T)\|_2\over\|g\|_2}
 \le C_A n^{-16}(\log n)^8+C_0n^{-c_0A_0}.               \tag{5.5}
\]

Choose \(c_0A_0>12\).  Even at the edge of C176's collar condition
\(\Lambda J^{7/2}\le n^2\), (0.3) is at most \(n^{-18}\),
so (5.5) makes the terminal heat error \(o(n^{-28})\).

But the preparation itself is not its terminal error.  If
\(\Phi_j(0)\) is C125's unit left growing functional transported to the
entrance, the actual target and one robust sufficient angular formulation
at the upper ledger scale (0.3) are, respectively,

\[
 \boxed{
 \left|\Phi_j(0)z_{\rm prep}\right|
       \lesssim n^{-28},
 \qquad
 {\left|\Phi_j(0)z_{\rm prep}\right|\over
        \|z_{\rm prep}\|_2}
       \lesssim {n^{-8}\over\Lambda J^{7/2}}.}            \tag{5.6}
\]

The second inequality is stronger than necessary when the actual prepared
norm is below (0.3).  It is a sufficient norm-to-projection requirement,
not a lower bound for a specially signed bundle.  Terminal
heat cancellation does not imply (5.6), because the prepared field is
present throughout the gain window and can couple through the full A2
linearization and nonlinear terms.  Equation (5.6) is the exact additional
relative gate for the order-one-rate branch.

### 5.1 The tuned C142 rate is scalar-compatible

On C142's viscous selector, suppressing fixed constants,

\[
                  \Lambda\asymp{\mu q^3\over\log n}.      \tag{5.7}
\]

With \(J\asymp\log h\asymp\log n\), (5.3) gives
\(\alpha=O(q^{-1})\), while

\[
 {S_{\rm prep}\over n^{-28}}
 \lesssim
 \mu n^{32}{J^{7/2}\over\log n}\longrightarrow0.        \tag{5.8}
\]

The convergence follows from C130: \(((n-1)!)^{-2}\) absorbs every fixed
polynomial and logarithm.  Hence this tuned subbranch needs no special
small angle in (5.6) merely to compare the **initial heat preparation
norm** with the seed.  This does not establish that the prepared component
survives the selector or C159 dynamics with the required state-class norm.

## 6. Exact surviving target

C178 rules out one false shortcut and supplies two constructive openings.

* A fixed nonzero compact profile under a nonnegative scalar ramp cannot
  be represented **exactly** by an \(L^2\) backward-heat initial datum,
  and no nonzero homogeneous viscous correction stays compactly supported
  for positive time.  Signed and moving multi-profile compatibility is not
  classified.
* Nevertheless, local Laplacian polynomials give compact, divergence-free
  entrance corrections with arbitrarily small terminal heat response.
  On the displayed heat-number gate, degree one already beats the C125
  terminal seed scale.
* Alternatively, a forward heat buffer gives an analytic-tailed,
  divergence-free entrance correction which cancels the pure-heat ramp
  response exactly and does not increase the raw \(L^2\) cost.  On
  \(\alpha=O(q^{-1})\) it changes the affine core only by \(e^{-cq}\),
  up to fixed derivative powers.
* For an order-one rate, the entrance correction is much larger than the
  \(n^{-28}\) microseed and owes the left-functional/Melnikov estimate
  (5.6).  On the tuned C142 rate its norm is factorially below the seed,
  giving a positive scalar subbranch.

The next proof must replace the heat semigroup in (1.2) by the actual
localized A2 evolution family and show that either prepared component, its
spatial tails, the collar Duhamel term, and their nonlinear interactions
obey C125's right/left relative norm and BAFL's active/wake split.  In the
analytic branch it must also propagate the exponentially perturbed core
cocycle.  C175/C177 determine whether a wake graph or autonomous reservoir
supplies that cancellation.  Until that estimate is proved, there is no
unforced one-cell map or blow-up result.

## 7. Verification boundary

`checks/compact_heat_preparation_c178.py` verifies the exact Fourier
Duhamel, analytic-buffer semigroup, and Taylor-remainder identities on
rational or deterministic test data, support and divergence commutation
algebra, the Poisson-tail bound, the C176 heat-number and preparation
exponents, the degree-one \(n^{-28}\) terminal ledger, and the
tuned-factorial comparison.  It cannot verify analytic unique
continuation, the Gaussian core estimate, the Gevrey tail hypothesis, the
actual A2 evolution family, pressure/heat image sums, C125, MCKC, LCE,
BAFL, or the unforced stage.
