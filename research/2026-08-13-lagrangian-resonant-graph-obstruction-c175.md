## 0. Claim boundary

C173 leaves one precise possibility for the broad part of MCKC(ii): perhaps
the moving \(q^2\)-to-\(q^3\) packet can be put on a finite-codimension
invariant graph after a Lagrangian/eikonal change of variables, so that its
retained \(b^2\) wake has only an \(O(b^3)\) active response without a
static order-\(q\) notch.

There is a useful exact reduction, but no automatic gain.

1. A common material eikonal preserves every material phase-label addition
   relation.
   In particular, the dangerous C140 relation

   \[
                    e_1-k_1=k_c                         \tag{0.1}
   \]

   remains an exact phase resonance under an arbitrary common smooth flow
   map.  Lagrangian frequency shear cannot integrate this interaction by
   parts in time.
2. On an allowed-class fixed A2 reference block, the corresponding
   Leray-projected first Duhamel response remains nonzero after heat is
   included.  Its scalar kernel has one sign and tends to \(3T/2\) as
   viscosity tends to zero.  The reference parent and wake share the C140
   labels, but are not the actual C116/C140 paired gate and its generated
   wake.  The calculation therefore refutes an automatic phase/heat gain,
   not every possible cancellation in the actual converter.
3. For a finite packet/Galerkin active--wake block, a full-domain graph
   \(a=K(t)w\) is invariant exactly when \(K\) solves a Riccati equation.
   The same statement applies to the PDE block under the common-domain
   hypothesis in Section 3.  Pairing that equation with diagonal right and
   left trajectories gives

   \[
      [\ell Kr]_{t_0}^{t_1}
       =\int_{t_0}^{t_1}\ell Br-\ell KCKr\,dt.       \tag{0.2}
   \]

   Hence a full-domain \(O(b)\)-tilted graph can exist only if every
   relevant first-order Melnikov transfer \(\int\ell Br\) is already
   \(O(b)\), up to the quadratic \(O(b^2)\) graph correction.  A graph over
   a restricted admissible wake subspace that is preserved by the diagonal
   wake propagator needs this estimate only on that subspace, but must
   additionally contain the actual retained wake.  The graph transform
   does not manufacture the missing factor; it repackages the corresponding
   restricted or full kernel estimate.

For a proposed active/wake splitting and wake class, this isolates the
**resonant invariant-graph Melnikov gate (RIGM)**:

> For the specified splitting, prove on a dynamically admissible wake class
> containing the actual retained packet an \(O(b)\) bound for the complete
> Leray--heat Melnikov operator by a signed amplitude/polarization
> cancellation, or keep every order-one resonant exposure to an \(O(b)\)
> action window, or spatially export the wake with the required global
> tails.

The explicit reference block rules out only the proposal that a common
eikonal or heat supplies the missing factor for every allowed block.  The
graph identity separately rules out a free gain from a uniformly small
graph after its splitting and domain are fixed.  RIGM is not asserted to
be necessary for every conceivable full converter before that data are
specified.  In particular, the result does not rule out a specially
designed multi-channel cancellation, a rapidly unfolded \(O(b)\)-time
converter, an order-one reclassification absorbed by a new endpoint
chart, or the C107 export branch.  No estimate for the actual C140 broad
packet, MCKC, BAFL, unforced stage, cascade, singularity, or Millennium
conclusion is claimed.

## 1. Common material phases preserve the resonant lattice

Let \(X(t,a)\) be any smooth flow map and write

\[
                    a(t,x)=X(t,\cdot)^{-1}(x).       \tag{1.1}
\]

For every material covector label \(k\), define

\[
                    \phi_k(t,x)=k\cdot a(t,x).       \tag{1.2}
\]

If \(X\) is generated by \(V\), then

\[
 D_t^V\phi_k=0,
 \qquad
 \nabla_x\phi_k=F^{-T}k,
 \qquad F=D_aX.                                    \tag{1.3}
\]

More importantly, linearity in the label gives the exact identities

\[
 \phi_{k+l}=\phi_k+\phi_l,
 \qquad
 \phi_{-k}=-\phi_k                                  \tag{1.4}
\]

without an affine-flow approximation.  Retain the C114/C140 labels

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 N&=(1,1,1),& k_1&=(1,-1,0),\\
 k_c&=(1,0,-1),& e_1&=(2,-1,-1).
 \end{aligned}                                      \tag{1.5}
\]

Since \(e_1=k_1+k_c\),

\[
 \boxed{\phi_{e_1}-\phi_{k_1}-\phi_{k_c}=0}          \tag{1.6}
\]

at every point and time at which the common chart exists.  Thus the
product of an \(e_1\) material wave with a \(-k_1\) material wave has
exactly the \(k_c\) phase.  There is no large relative phase derivative
from the C154/C159 covector shear.

The same observation applies to the whole C161 translation star.  If
\(s_{bc}\) is a source label and \(g_a\) a charge shift, then

\[
                 \phi_{s_{bc}+g_a}
                 =\phi_{s_{bc}}+\phi_{g_a}          \tag{1.7}
\]

simultaneously for all \(q^3\) daughters.  An eikonal frame is still useful
for removing the large transport part of the generator, but it does not
make these resonant source--gate or wake--return edges oscillatory.

This statement concerns a *common* flow map.  Assigning different physical
flows to different colors creates phase mismatch, overlap, and equation
residuals which must be charged separately; it is not a counterexample to
(1.6).

## 2. An allowed-class A2 Leray--heat first response has no automatic gain

Put

\[
                       A_*=(1,1,-2).                \tag{2.1}
\]

Then

\[
 \begin{gathered}
 A_*\cdot k_1=0,
 \qquad A_*\cdot e_1=3,
 \qquad N\cdot e_1=N\cdot k_c=0,\\
 |k_1|^2=|k_c|^2=2,
 \qquad |e_1|^2=6.                                 \tag{2.2}
 \end{gathered}
\]

For constants \(\alpha,\beta\), consider the exact heat-decaying parent
plane wave and a heat-decaying wake input

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 V(t,x)&=\alpha e^{-2\nu t}A_*\cos(k_1\cdot x),\\
 W_{\mathrm{in}}(t,x)&=\beta e^{-6\nu t}N\cos(e_1\cdot x).
 \end{aligned}                                     \tag{2.3}
\]

The parent is itself an exact unforced Navier--Stokes solution because a
single divergence-free plane wave has zero self-advection.  The wake in
(2.3) is used as the input of the first linear Duhamel/Born response; it is
not asserted to be a closed solution of the full infinite sideband ladder.
This is the allowed-class test already isolated in C171.  Although its
labels are the C140 labels, \(V\) is not the actual C116/C140 paired gate,
\(W_{\mathrm{in}}\) is not its generated wake, and no invariant subblock
of the actual converter is asserted.

The ordered interactions are exactly

\[
 (W_{\mathrm{in}}\cdot\nabla)V=0,
 \qquad
 (V\cdot\nabla)W_{\mathrm{in}}
 =-{3\alpha\beta\over2}e^{-8\nu t}N
 \{\sin((e_1+k_1)\cdot x)+\sin(k_c\cdot x)\}.      \tag{2.4}
\]

Both output labels are perpendicular to \(N\), so Leray projection leaves
the displayed polarization unchanged.  In the linearized equation
\(\partial_tW=\nu\Delta W-P(V\cdot\nabla W+W\cdot\nabla V)\), the signed
coupling per unit wake coefficient at \(k_c\), and its magnitude, are

\[
                  B_0={3\alpha\over2},
 \qquad |B_0|={3|\alpha|\over2}.                   \tag{2.5}
\]

Starting from zero \(k_c\)-child amplitude, the signed active first
response at time \(T\) is

\[
 \boxed{
 a_{\mathrm{Born}}(T)
 ={3\alpha\beta\over2}e^{-2\nu T}
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle {1-e^{-6\nu T}\over6\nu},&\nu>0,\\[2mm]
 T,&\nu=0.
 \end{cases}}                                      \tag{2.6}
\]

For \(\alpha=\beta=1\), every scalar factor in (2.6) is positive.  Heat
damps this reference response but does not provide signed cancellation.
If \(6\nu T\le1\), then

\[
 {1-e^{-6\nu T}\over6\nu}\ge {T\over2},
 \qquad
 |a_{\mathrm{Born}}(T)|\ge {3T\over4}e^{-2\nu T}.  \tag{2.7}
\]

On the C127 schedule, with \(n=j+1\), the normalized viscosity is
\(\mu_j=\nu((n-1)!)^{-2}\).  Thus for \(\alpha=\beta=1\) and a fixed
positive action interval the coefficient in (2.6) tends to \(3T/2\),
while \(b=n^{-2}\to0\).  This allowed-class reference edge is therefore
not automatically an \(O(b)\) block.

Equation (2.6) is a first-response statement for the reference block.
Other polarizations and modes in the actual converter may change or
cancel it.  Once a proposed C140 splitting and admissible wake class are
fixed, proving the required signed cancellation uniformly over that class
is the RIGM obligation; it is not something excluded by (2.6).

## 3. Moving projections and the exact finite-block graph equation

First take finite packet/Galerkin active and wake spaces \({\cal A}\) and
\({\cal W}\), and choose any differentiable moving frame which identifies
the desired fibers with those fixed spaces.  The frame may include material
phases, moving Leray fibers, heat weights, and polarization bases.  The
linearized evolution in that frame has the block form

\[
 {d\over dt}\binom a w
 =\begin{pmatrix}A(t)&B(t)\\ C(t)&D(t)\end{pmatrix}
   \binom a w.                                      \tag{3.1}
\]

For a physical projector \(P(t)\), the block \(B\) contains both the
generator coupling and the connection term corresponding to
\(\dot P Q+P{\cal L}Q\).  Hence (3.1) does not omit the moving-projector
exposure isolated in C140.

Equations (3.1)--(4.4) below are exact for these finite blocks and for
bounded strongly differentiable block families on Banach spaces.  To use
them directly for the unbounded Navier--Stokes PDE generator, assume that
\(A,B,C,D,K\) preserve a common dense invariant domain on which the
displayed products and derivatives are defined, and that the resulting
diagonal evolution families have the stated bounds.  Proving such domain
and closure properties in the localized packet norm is part of RIGM/BAFL;
without them the PDE reading of the differential Riccati equation is only
formal.  The mild propagator form (6.2) is the eventual PDE target.

Let a candidate invariant graph be

\[
                         a=K(t)w.                   \tag{3.2}
\]

Substitution into (3.1) gives the exact Riccati equation

\[
 \boxed{
 K'=AK+B-KD-KCK.}                                  \tag{3.3}
\]

In particular, if a proposed family has

\[
 \|K_b\|+\lambda^{-1}\|K_b'\|=O(b),
 \qquad
 \|A\|+\|C\|+\|D\|=O(\lambda),                    \tag{3.4}
\]

then (3.3) forces \(\|B\|=O(b\lambda)\).  An
order-\(\lambda\) resonant matrix element such as (2.4) rules out that
particular uniformly \(C^1\)-small graph.  Allowing a rapidly varying
graph avoids the pointwise conclusion, but it does not avoid the
integrated identity below.

There is a tautological order-one graph: transport the wake subspace by
the exact full propagator.  In that moving decomposition \(B=0\).  Its
endpoint need not agree with the prescribed active/wake split, however,
and its slope relative to that split is precisely the accumulated Duhamel
transfer.  Declaring this transported subspace to be the wake therefore
does not prove the \(O(b)\) endpoint alignment required by BAFL.

## 4. The finite-block graph--Melnikov identity

Let \(r(t)\in{\cal W}\) and \(\ell(t)\in{\cal A}^*\) solve the diagonal
equations

\[
                         r'=Dr,
 \qquad
                         \ell'=-\ell A.             \tag{4.1}
\]

Differentiate \(\ell Kr\), use (3.3), and cancel the diagonal terms.  One
obtains exactly

\[
 \boxed{
 {d\over dt}(\ell Kr)=\ell Br-\ell KCKr.}          \tag{4.2}
\]

Consequently,

\[
 \boxed{
 [\ell Kr]_{t_0}^{t_1}
 =\int_{t_0}^{t_1}\ell Br\,dt
  -\int_{t_0}^{t_1}\ell KCKr\,dt.}                \tag{4.3}
\]

Suppose on the interval that

\[
 \|K\|\le\varepsilon,
 \qquad \|\ell\|,\|r\|\le M.
\]

Then (4.3) implies the necessary bound

\[
 \boxed{
 \left|\int_{t_0}^{t_1}\ell Br\,dt\right|
 \le2M^2\varepsilon
   +M^2\varepsilon^2\int_{t_0}^{t_1}\|C(t)\|\,dt.} \tag{4.4}
\]

Thus \(\varepsilon=O(b)\) requires every such first-order Melnikov pairing
to be \(O(b)\), provided the diagonal trajectories and integrated \(C\)
remain uniformly controlled.  The nonlinear graph term is only
\(O(b^2)\).  This is the rigorous sense in which a uniformly small
invariant graph requires the missing kernel estimate at first order.  The
converse is not claimed: a small first-order Melnikov operator alone does
not construct a solution of the Riccati equation.

Block-diagonal changes of active and wake frames do not change this
conclusion: they transform \(B,\ell,r,K\) covariantly and leave the scalar
identity (4.3) invariant.  A common eikonal is such a block-diagonal frame
change by material labels.  A transformation that mixes active and wake
spaces can set \(B\) to zero only by moving the graph slope into the
definition of the endpoint splitting.

## 5. Exact terminal-dark graph on the allowed-class heat block

Normalize the reference parent to \(\alpha=1\), measure \(w\) as the
coefficient of \(N\cos(e_1\cdot x)\), and measure \(a\) as the coefficient
of \(N\sin(k_c\cdot x)\).  The scalar block underlying (2.6) then has

\[
 A=-2\nu,
 \qquad D=-6\nu,
 \qquad C=0,
 \qquad B(t)=B_0e^{-2\nu t},
 \quad B_0={3\over2}.                              \tag{5.1}
\]

Impose terminal darkness \(K(T)=0\).  Equation (3.3) has the exact
solution

\[
 K(t)=-B_0
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle {e^{-2\nu t}-e^{4\nu t-6\nu T}\over6\nu},
       &\nu>0,\\[2mm]
 T-t,&\nu=0.
 \end{cases}                                      \tag{5.2}
\]

In particular,

\[
 \boxed{
 |K(0)|=B_0
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle {1-e^{-6\nu T}\over6\nu},&\nu>0,\\[2mm]
 T,&\nu=0.
 \end{cases}}                                     \tag{5.3}
\]

For \(6\nu T\le1\),

\[
                         |K(0)|\ge {3T\over4}.     \tag{5.4}
\]

Thus the terminal-dark graph for this reference scalar block over a fixed
positive resonant action interval has order-one slope as \(b\to0\).  A
wake of size \(b^2\) lying on this scalar graph carries an active component
of size \(b^2\), not \(b^3\).  This is not the terminal graph of the actual
C140 coupled ladder.

The alternatives are visible directly in (5.3):

* make the exposure interval \(T=O(b)\), as in a genuinely just-in-time
  microgate;
* make the projected coupling \(B_0=O(b)\) by polarization or spatial
  overlap;
* cancel the Melnikov integral with other signed channels; or
* export the wake so that the complete physical \(B\), including pressure
  and heat tails, is \(O(b)\).

Fast oscillation could also reduce a nonresonant integral.  Equation (1.6)
shows that the common-flow phase of the named A2 edge is exactly resonant,
so that option must come from an additional amplitude/control phase, not
from Lagrangian transport itself.

## 6. The broad-packet operator that remains to be estimated

Fix a proposed active/wake splitting in the actual eikonal frame.  Let
\({\cal U}_A(t,s)\) and \({\cal U}_W(t,s)\) denote its diagonal active and
wake propagators.  Assume their norms are
uniformly controlled in the BAFL weights, and assume the relevant graph
satisfies

\[
 \sup_{0\le t\le T}\|K(t)\|\lesssim b,
 \qquad \int_0^T\|C(t)\|\,dt\lesssim1.             \tag{6.1}
\]

The first-order broad-packet transfer is

\[
 {\cal M}_q(T,0)
 =\int_0^T{\cal U}_A(T,s)B_q(s){\cal U}_W(s,0)\,ds. \tag{6.2}
\]

For a graph over the entire relevant wake packet, (4.3)--(4.4) require,
in the corresponding weighted packet norms,

\[
                         \boxed{\|{\cal M}_q\|\lesssim b.}  \tag{6.3}
\]

There is a distinct restricted-domain alternative.  Let
\({\cal W}_{\rm adm}(t)\) be a finite-codimension admissible wake subspace
such that \({\cal U}_W(t,s){\cal W}_{\rm adm}(s)\subset
{\cal W}_{\rm adm}(t)\), and suppose the graph/Riccati identity is defined
on these diagonal trajectories.  The construction must separately prove
that the corresponding graph is carried by the full coupled dynamics.
Then the necessary statement is only

\[
 \boxed{
  \|{\cal M}_q|_{{\cal W}_{\rm adm}(0)}\|\lesssim b,
  \qquad w_{\rm physical}(0)\in{\cal W}_{\rm adm}(0).}       \tag{6.4}
\]

This distinction is load-bearing.  For \(A=C=D=0\) and nonzero rank-one
\(B\), the subspace \(\ker B\) is finite-codimensional and exactly dark
although the full norm \(\|TB\|\) is order one.  Such a restricted graph
helps only if the actual retained wake, including its curvature,
localization, and pressure components, lies in the transported dark
subspace.  Conversely, a full-domain graph may encode correlations among
many packet coefficients, but unless its slope is \(O(b)\), multiplying it
by the retained \(b^2\) wake produces an \(O(b^2)\) active component.  If an
order-one graph component is instead declared part of the intended
endpoint chart, the endpoint map must prove that this reclassification
preserves the next pump and its \(b^3\) active tolerance.

The material identities (1.6)--(1.7) say that the named label interaction
cannot be made nonresonant solely by the common C154/C159 flow.  Equation
(2.6) shows that Leray plus heat does not cancel the allowed-class
reference edge.  These facts do not prove that the actual C140 operator is
bright on every admissible wake class.  Rather, if a proposed class
contains uncanceled versions of these resonant directions, a proof of
(6.3), or of the physically admissible restricted statement (6.4), must
expose one of the following genuinely dynamical structures:

1. a signed full-polarization cancellation in the actual \(q^2\)-to-\(q^3\)
   operator, stable under curvature and localization;
2. an unfolded schedule in which every uncanceled resonant exposure has
   total action \(O(b)\);
3. a spatial-export estimate for the complete velocity/pressure/heat
   evolution; or
4. an endpoint chart which absorbs an order-one graph tilt without
   violating the BAFL active channel.

This is RIGM: a sharp necessary amplitude condition for the current
uniformly small-graph strategy after its splitting and wake class are
fixed.  It is not asserted as a splitting-independent necessary condition
for every full converter, and it is not a request for another geometry.

## 7. Verification boundary

The dependency-free checker verifies:

* all A2 frequency, polarization, Leray, and heat exponents in Section 2;
* exact preservation of the named resonance under nontrivial rational
  material covector maps;
* the finite-matrix Riccati and graph--Melnikov identities over the
  rationals;
* the terminal-dark scalar graph formula and its inviscid limit; and
* the schedule comparison showing that a fixed heat-block coefficient is
  not \(O(b)\).

It cannot identify the reference block with the actual C140 pump/wake,
verify cancellation of the actual broad operator (6.2), prove that the
physical wake belongs to a proposed admissible subspace, or verify
localization, pressure-image sums, packet residence, endpoint coherence,
MCKC, LCE, BAFL, or an unforced Navier--Stokes stage.
