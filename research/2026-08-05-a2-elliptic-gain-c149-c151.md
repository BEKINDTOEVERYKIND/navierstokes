## 0. Claim boundary

This note does not introduce a new geometry. It stays on the C114--C148
\(A_2\) pump and attacks the positive-cocycle part of LBRG that C148 left
open.

There is a genuine positive result. A nonsymmetric three-root member of
the exact C121 Beltrami family has a vertical periodic ray whose Kelvin
covector is bounded and periodic and whose divergence-free amplitude has a
simple expanding Floquet multiplier. This avoids the material-envelope
stretch that killed the generic affine selector in C143.

There is also a sharp instantaneous obstruction. Normal charge removes the
quadratic term from the retained band, but it does not remove derivatives
from the quadratic wake and cubic return. An explicit pair of limiting
unstable rays has wake \(Qb^2\) and a nonzero growing-line return \(Q^2b^3\)
in the frozen Euler symbol. Thus no \(Q\)-free pointwise multilinear defect
estimate can hold uniformly on the whole two-ray bundle. If this channel
remains coherently aligned during the gain interval, the corresponding
scalar model reaches its natural nonlinear threshold at amplitude
\(Q^{-1}\), far before the C147 target. The exact calculation alone does
not establish that temporal coherence.

We call this exact instantaneous channel the **derivative-amplified cubic
return (DACR)**. It is not a universal no-go for a specially phased packet.
A stage can still survive through temporal or all-packet cancellation, or
by putting the nonlinear depletion/conversion into the leading trajectory.
Neither cancellation nor a coherent time-integrated lower bound is proved
here.

## 1. C149: an exact elliptic Kelvin instability on the C121 pump

Use the existing roots

\[
 r_1=(1,-1,0),\qquad r_2=(0,1,-1),\qquad
 r_3=-r_1-r_2=(-1,0,1),\qquad N=(1,1,1).
 \tag{1.1}
\]

For \(0<\delta<1\), put

\[
 f_\delta(x)=\cos(r_1\cdot x)+\cos(r_2\cdot x)
              +\delta\cos(r_3\cdot x),
 \qquad
 U_\delta=N\times\nabla f_\delta-\sqrt2 f_\delta N.
 \tag{1.2}
\]

Every root is orthogonal to \(N\) and has squared length \(2\). Hence

\[
 \nabla\times U_\delta=\sqrt2\,U_\delta,
 \tag{1.3}
\]

so \(P_0e^{-2\nu t}U_\delta\) is an exact unforced Navier--Stokes
background at \(K=1\). The \(K\)-rescaled background has heat factor
\(e^{-2\nu K^2t}\).

At \(x=0\), \(\nabla f_\delta=0\) and the trajectory is vertical because

\[
 U_\delta(0)=-\sqrt2(2+\delta)N.
 \tag{1.4}
\]

All root phases remain zero along this trajectory. In the orthonormal
basis (which is left-handed: \(e_x\times e_y=-e_z\))

\[
 e_x={r_1+r_2\over\sqrt2},\qquad
 e_y={r_1-r_2\over\sqrt6},\qquad
 e_z={N\over\sqrt3},
 \tag{1.5}
\]

the velocity gradient on it is

\[
 \nabla U_\delta
 =\gamma A_\epsilon,
 \qquad
 A_\epsilon=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 0&-(1+\epsilon)&0\\
 1-\epsilon&0&0\\
 0&0&0
 \end{pmatrix},
 \tag{1.6}
\]

where

\[
 \gamma=\sqrt3(2+\delta),\qquad
 \epsilon={1-\delta\over2+\delta},\qquad
 \delta={1-2\epsilon\over1+\epsilon}.
 \tag{1.7}
\]

Thus \(0<\epsilon<1/2\) keeps all three pump roots nonzero.

### 1.1 Kelvin system and periodic covector

Use \(s=\gamma t\) for the unit-amplitude inviscid background. The
geometric-optics covector and divergence-free amplitude solve

\[
 \xi'=-A_\epsilon^T\xi,
 \qquad
 a'=-A_\epsilon a
       +2{\xi\over|\xi|^2}(\xi\cdot A_\epsilon a),
 \qquad \xi\cdot a=0.
 \tag{1.8}
\]

Starting from \(\xi(0)=(1,0,r)\), let
\(\omega=\sqrt{1-\epsilon^2}\). Then exactly

\[
 \xi(s)=\left(
 \cos(\omega s),
 \sqrt{{1+\epsilon\over1-\epsilon}}\sin(\omega s),
 r\right),
 \qquad S_\epsilon={2\pi\over\omega}.
 \tag{1.9}
\]

The frequency deformation is therefore uniformly bounded for fixed small
\(\epsilon\). This is the key difference from C143's hyperbolic affine
selector.

### 1.2 Exact first variation of the amplitude monodromy

Let

\[
 r_0={1\over\sqrt3},\qquad r=r_0+c\epsilon,
 \tag{1.10}
\]

and use the initial transverse frame

\[
 E_1=(0,1,0),\qquad
 E_2={(-r,0,1)\over\sqrt{1+r^2}}.
 \tag{1.11}
\]

Write

\[
 J=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1&0\\1&0&0\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 S=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1&0\\-1&0&0\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad A_\epsilon=J+\epsilon S.
 \tag{1.12}
\]

After the rotating change \(\eta=R(-s)\xi\), \(b=R(-s)a\), set
\(S_s=R(-s)SR(s)\). The equations become

\[
 \eta'=-\epsilon S_s\eta,
 \qquad
 b'=C(\eta,\epsilon,s)b,
 \tag{1.13}
\]

with

\[
 S_s=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 -\sin2s&-\cos2s&0\\
 -\cos2s& \sin2s&0\\
 0&0&0
 \end{pmatrix},
 \tag{1.14}
\]

\[
 C(\eta,\epsilon,s)
 =-2J+2P(\eta)J
  +\epsilon\{-S_s+2P(\eta)S_s\},
 \qquad
 P(\eta)={\eta\eta^T\over|\eta|^2}.
 \tag{1.15}
\]

At first order,

\[
 \eta_0=(1,0,1/\sqrt3),\qquad
 \eta_1=\left({1-\cos2s\over2},{\sin2s\over2},c\right).
 \tag{1.16}
\]

The unperturbed amplitude matrix is

\[
 C_*=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 0&1/2&0\\
 -2&0&0\\
 0&-\sqrt3/2&0
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 e^{C_*s}=I+\sin s\,C_*+(1-\cos s)C_*^2.
 \tag{1.17}
\]

In particular \(e^{2\pi C_*}=I\). Differentiating (1.15), inserting
(1.16), and extracting the zero Fourier mode in

\[
 E^T\int_0^{2\pi}
 e^{-C_*s}C_1(s;c)e^{C_*s}\,ds\,E
 \tag{1.18}
\]

gives the exact monodromy expansion

\[
 M(\epsilon,r_0+c\epsilon)
 =I-\pi\epsilon
 \begin{pmatrix}
 0&\displaystyle {15\over8}-{3\sqrt3\over2}c\\[2mm]
 \displaystyle {3\over8}+{3\sqrt3\over2}c&0
 \end{pmatrix}
 +O(\epsilon^2).
 \tag{1.19}
\]

There is no first-order period correction because
\(S_\epsilon=2\pi+O(\epsilon^2)\). The derivative of the frame (1.11)
also makes no transverse endpoint contribution because it is longitudinal
and \(e^{2\pi C_*}=I\).

Choose

\[
 c_0={1\over2\sqrt3}.
 \tag{1.20}
\]

Then

\[
 M=I-{9\pi\over8}\epsilon
 \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}+O(\epsilon^2),
 \tag{1.21}
\]

so its two multipliers are

\[
 \rho_\pm=1\pm{9\pi\over8}\epsilon+O(\epsilon^2).
 \tag{1.22}
\]

The expanding exponent per unit inertial time for the unit-frequency,
unit-amplitude normalization is therefore

\[
 \boxed{\sigma_\epsilon={9\over16}\gamma\epsilon
        +O(\gamma\epsilon^2)>0.}
 \tag{1.23}
\]

This coefficient uses the standardized parameter
\(\epsilon=(1-\delta)/(2+\delta)\). If instead
\(\eta=\delta-1\), then \(\eta=-3\epsilon+O(\epsilon^2)\); the same
minimal-return splitting has coefficient \(3\pi|\eta|/8\) at first order.
The two statements are identical after this parameter change.

For the inviscid or instantaneously frozen physical background
\(P_0U_\delta(Kx)\), the exponent per unit physical time is

\[
 \sigma_{\epsilon,\mathrm{phys}}
 =P_0K\left\{{9\over16}\gamma\epsilon
              +O(\gamma\epsilon^2)\right\}.
 \tag{1.24}
\]

More generally, both off-diagonal factors in (1.19) are positive when

\[
 -{1\over4\sqrt3}<c<{5\over4\sqrt3}.
 \tag{1.25}
\]

Analytic dependence of the ODE then gives an open instability tongue and,
on every compact sub-tongue for sufficiently small fixed \(\epsilon\), a
simple expanding line separated uniformly from the neutral boundary.

### 1.3 Heat ledger and what is not yet proved

For the exact heat-decaying pump, use inertial time
\(\theta=P_0Kt\) and \(\mu=\nu K/P_0\). The inviscid part of the Kelvin
cocycle uses the exact reparametrization

\[
 s(\theta)=\gamma\int_0^\theta e^{-2\mu\tau}\,d\tau
 ={\gamma(1-e^{-2\mu\theta})\over2\mu}.
 \tag{1.26}
\]

For \(\theta=O(\log n)\), C127 gives

\[
 \mu\log n\to0,
 \qquad
 \mu q^2\log n\to0.
 \tag{1.27}
\]

Thus pump decay and heat on a \(qK\)-frequency packet are both negligible
at the scalar level, while (1.23) supplies \(O(\log n)\) gain in
\(O(\log n)\) inertial time.

The selected ray is not stationary. At every critical point of
\(f_\delta\),

\[
 U_\delta=-\sqrt2 f_\delta N,\qquad
 \operatorname{tr}\nabla^2f_\delta=\Delta f_\delta=-2f_\delta.
 \tag{1.28}
\]

An elliptic critical point has definite nonzero Hessian, so \(f_\delta\ne0\)
and its axial speed is nonzero. For fixed small \(\epsilon>0\), during the
required gain interval this ray travels \(O(\log n)\) parent lengths. The
global periodic C121 background
supports that orbit after advective rephasing, but one localized parent
must supply a co-moving or length-\(O(\log n)\) core. This is an open
localization/export obligation, not an energy no-go. A different possible
repair remains inside the same geometry: a regular closed component of
\(\{f_\delta=0\}\) has zero axial drift and could conceivably carry its own
positive periodic Kelvin cocycle. That cocycle has not been computed.

Consequently C149 is not yet a periodic PDE eigenpacket. It still requires
a long-time microlocal/Egorov construction, control away from the central
vertical ray, orbit/core matching, packet-edge estimates, and C125
relative control.

## 2. C150: exact normal-charge quadratic darkness

Every existing pump root has zero \(N\)-charge. Write

\[
 \mathcal F_m=\{v:\widehat v(k)=0\text{ unless }
 k=mN+br_1+cr_2\text{ for some }b,c\in\mathbb Z\}.
 \tag{2.1}
\]

The exact C121 linearization preserves each \(\mathcal F_m\), while the
Navier--Stokes bilinear map obeys

\[
 B(\mathcal F_m,\mathcal F_{m'})
 \subseteq\mathcal F_{m+m'}.
 \tag{2.2}
\]

Let

\[
 S=[m_0-\Delta,m_0+\Delta]\cap\mathbb Z,
 \qquad m_0>3\Delta,
 \tag{2.3}
\]

and let \(H\) be the real retained space with charges in \(S\cup(-S)\).
The quadratic charge sets are contained in

\[
 \pm(S+S)\ \cup\ (S-S),
 \tag{2.4}
\]

which is disjoint from \(S\cup(-S)\) under (2.3). Therefore

\[
 \boxed{P_HB(v_H,v_H)=0.}
 \tag{2.5}
\]

Because the pump linearization preserves charge, it cannot linearly return
the quadratic complement to \(H\). Degree three is the earliest
support-allowed return, through combinations such as
\((S+S)+(-S)\). This is a support grading only: it neither forces a
nonzero cubic coefficient for every datum nor removes derivative factors.
The concrete C144 charge band \(S_q=\{4q,\ldots,5q-1\}\) satisfies the same
separation directly: \(S_q+S_q\), \(S_q-S_q\), and their negatives are
disjoint from \(S_q\cup(-S_q)\).

## 3. C151: the derivative-amplified cubic return

The derivative loss is not merely a crude norm estimate. It is nonzero
on an explicit pair of limiting unstable rays.

Work in the orthonormal coordinates of (1.5), and set

\[
 r={1\over\sqrt3},\qquad
 k=(1,0,r),\qquad \ell=(0,1,r),
 \tag{3.1}
\]

\[
 a=\left({1\over2\sqrt2},{1\over\sqrt2},
          -{\sqrt3\over2\sqrt2}\right),
 \qquad
 b=\left({1\over\sqrt2},{1\over2\sqrt2},
          -{\sqrt3\over2\sqrt2}\right).
 \tag{3.2}
\]

Then \(|a|=|b|=1\), \(k\cdot a=\ell\cdot b=0\). These are the
\(\epsilon\downarrow0\) expanding directions at horizontal phases \(0\)
and \(\pi/2\).

For divergence-free inputs define the symmetric Leray interaction

\[
 \mathcal S(p,A;q,B)
 =P_{p+q}\{(A\cdot q)B+(B\cdot p)A\}.
 \tag{3.3}
\]

Exact arithmetic gives

\[
 c:=\mathcal S(k,a;\ell,b)
 ={3\over10}(1,1,-\sqrt3),
 \tag{3.4}
\]

and

\[
 \mathcal S(k+\ell,c;-\ell,b)
 ={c\over2\sqrt2},
 \qquad
 a\cdot\left(-{c\over2\sqrt2}\right)=-{9\over40}.
 \tag{3.5}
\]

The same-sign self and first-difference interactions vanish, while the
opposite pairs vanish before the zero-mode Leray projection. Consequently,
for the real four-mode datum on \(\pm k,\pm\ell\), the Euler symbol
\(-i\mathcal S\) creates wake \(-ic\) at \(k+\ell\) and the conjugate
\(+ic\) at \(-k-\ell\). Its next interaction with the \(-\ell\) parent
returns \(-c/(2\sqrt2)\) at \(k\), with nonzero projection \(-9/40\) onto
the growing direction \(a\); the negative-frequency path is its reality
partner rather than a cancellation.

After scaling all wavevectors by \(Q\) and taking input amplitude \(A\),
the exact homogeneities are

\[
 \text{quadratic wake}\asymp QA^2,
 \qquad
 \text{growing-line cubic return}\asymp Q^2A^3.
 \tag{3.6}
\]

The limiting \(\epsilon=0\) pair occurs on actual same-charge lattice
sequences. For example, with nearest-integer choices

\[
 p_m=mN+B_m(r_1+r_2),\quad
 B_m=\operatorname{round}(3m/\sqrt2),
 \tag{3.7}
\]

\[
 q_m=mN+D_m(r_1-r_2),\quad
 D_m=\operatorname{round}(3m/\sqrt6),
 \tag{3.8}
\]

one has, in the basis (1.5),

\[
 {p_m\over3m}\to k,\qquad {q_m\over3m}\to\ell.
 \tag{3.9}
\]

Projecting \(a,b\) onto \(p_m^\perp,q_m^\perp\) preserves convergence.
Homogeneity and continuity then retain the nonzero quadratic and cubic
coefficients on an integer \(m\sim B\sim D\sim Q\) sequence. Equations
(3.7)--(3.9) certify the limiting symbol, not exact finite-\(\epsilon\)
Floquet eigenpackets. The finite-\(\epsilon\) direction retuning can also be
made explicit. Put

\[
 r_\epsilon=r_0+c_0\epsilon,\qquad
 \alpha_\epsilon=\sqrt{{1+\epsilon\over1-\epsilon}},\qquad
 Q_m={\sqrt3m\over r_\epsilon},
\]

and replace \(B_m,D_m\) by

\[
 B_m^{(\epsilon)}=\operatorname{round}
   {\sqrt3m\over\sqrt2r_\epsilon},\qquad
 D_m^{(\epsilon)}=\operatorname{round}
   {\alpha_\epsilon m\over\sqrt2r_\epsilon}.
\]

Then the corresponding same-charge lattice vectors, divided by \(Q_m\),
converge at rate \(O(m^{-1})\) to
\((1,0,r_\epsilon)\) and \((0,\alpha_\epsilon,r_\epsilon)\), the two
quarter-period covectors. For every sufficiently small fixed
\(\epsilon>0\), the simple expanding lines and this nonzero interaction
persist by continuity. This is still a local-symbol asymptotic: realizing
those directions as an evolving torus packet requires the open microlocal
construction.

This proves that no \(Q\)-free *instantaneous* quadratic/cubic defect
estimate can hold uniformly on the whole unstable two-ray bundle.

For this raw two-ray channel, relative to linear strain growth \(O(A)\),
the instantaneous cubic correction has size

\[
 Q^2A^2.
 \tag{3.10}
\]

It becomes order one at the raw threshold

\[
 \boxed{A\asymp Q^{-1}.}
 \tag{3.11}
\]

For C147, \(Q\asymp q=n^8\) and the point seed is
\(A_0=n^{-16}=q^{-2}\). If the displayed channel remains coherently
aligned and uncancelled during growth, it reaches (3.11) after the factor
\(q=n^8\), before the proposed factor \(n^{26}\). This is not yet a
conclusion about the specially constructed \(q^3\)-mode packet.

Indeed, in the conditional scalar coherence model
\(A(t)=A_0e^{\sigma t}\), with a fixed signed cubic coefficient, the
backward-weighted cubic relative integral has the scale

\[
 {1\over A(T)}\int_0^T
 e^{\sigma(T-t)}Q^2A(t)^3\,dt
 ={Q^2\over2\sigma}\{A(T)^2-A_0^2\}.
 \tag{3.12}
\]

At the C147 final point amplitude \(A(T)=n^{10}\), this is of order
\(q^2n^{20}=n^{36}\), not \(o(1)\).

The exact result is the nonzero instantaneous DACR symbol (3.4)--(3.6);
(3.12) is its conditional coherent scalar extrapolation. The one-time
symbol does not prove that its signed projection remains aligned through a
Floquet period or the full \(O(\log n)\) interval. Floquet averaging,
temporal phase cancellation, or cross-channel packet cancellation could
change the accumulated response. Its safe conclusion is therefore narrow:

- it refutes a \(Q\)-free pointwise bandwise defect estimate and requires
  any full-gain LBRG proof to cancel, propagate, or include DACR;
- the threshold (3.11) and \(n^{36}\) ledger apply conditionally when the
  displayed channel stays coherently aligned;
- it does not refute an existence construction whose packet has an exact
  temporal or multilinear cancellation; and
- it does not refute a nonlinear leading model that incorporates pump
  depletion and broad-band conversion, analogous to C118/C124.

## 4. Updated one-cell target

The positive elliptic cocycle settles one local principal ray without
hyperbolic frequency stretch. The next theorem is no
longer merely “find a positive band.” It is:

> **Nonlinear localized broad-band relative gain.** Construct, on the
> exact C121 pump, a localized high-normal-charge packet whose elliptic
> principal cocycle supplies the required gain while either cancelling
> DACR throughout the packet or incorporating the \(QA^2,Q^2A^3\)
> dynamics into a controlled depletion/conversion orbit; prove the C125
> backward-weighted residual relative to the \(n^{-28}\) seed, retain the
> \(n^{-4}\) wake budget, and land in a localized C121-like next-pump core.

This theorem remains open. No unforced one-cell Navier--Stokes stage,
cascade, blow-up solution, or Millennium conclusion is claimed.
