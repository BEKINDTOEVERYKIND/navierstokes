## 0. Claim boundary

C163 reduces the first charge step, after a source-dependent positive
similarity, to a family of two-level systems with rates

\[
 \Lambda_i^2=A_i^2\sum_j w_j R(\phi_i,y_{ij}),
 \qquad w_j=|\gamma_j|^2\geq 0.                    \tag{0.1}
\]

This note gives three new finite-dimensional conclusions on that same
geometry and imports one exact full-ladder obstruction from C164.

1. No nonzero static choice of shared nonnegative gate intensities can make
   the C163 squared rate independent of angle on a fixed-radius open source
   arc.  The coefficient of \(\sin(2\phi)\) is rigid and cannot be changed by
   the gate heights.
2. C164's exact tangential full-charge branch is only multiplication by a
   pointwise phase.  It can spread one Fourier charge among many charges but
   cannot increase its physical point amplitude; this is cited below rather
   than claimed a second time here.
3. On a finite independent weighted-star model, one common short time can
   equalize the daughter coefficients after bounded source preweighting; the
   price is an order-one source remnant.
4. If one is allowed to prescribe a common time-dependent gate envelope and
   phase, then every weighted two-level block whose rate lies in a fixed
   compact interval \([\lambda_-,\lambda_+]\subset(0,\infty)\) can be
   transferred, up to a generally rate-dependent phase, with state error
   \(O(J^{-1})\) in time \(J\).  The proof is an elementary quantitative
   adiabatic argument with an explicit constant.

The fourth result is **prescribed-control algebra only**.  Its normalized
envelope area is \(2J/3\) (and a block of rate \(\lambda\) has transverse
action \(2\lambda J/3\)), whereas the C146/C161 unfolded ledger has \(J=b^{-1}\)
factors of angle \(O(b)\) and hence only \(O(1)\) total action.  Scaling the
chirped Hamiltonian by \(b\) leaves effective adiabatic time \(bJ=1\), so it
does not give an \(O(b)\) error in that ledger.  No unforced pump is shown to
generate the envelope or phase, and the C163 weighted metric is not converted
to physical Fourier energy.

## 1. The rigid \(\sin(2\phi)\) coefficient

Put

\[
 x=\sin(2\phi),\qquad
 \kappa(y)={y^2(y-2)\over4(y^2+2y+4)}.              \tag{1.1}
\]

On C163's positive-return regime (in particular its certified
\(-1\leq y<\infty\) signed-height band away from \(h=0\)), the identities
\(h^2=(\cos\phi-\sin\phi)^2=1-x\) and
\(\ell^2=(\cos\phi+\sin\phi)^2=1+x\) turn its directed return into

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 R(\phi,y)
 &= {1-x\over2}\{1+(1+x)\kappa(y)\}\\
 &= {1\over2}\{1+\kappa(y)-x-\kappa(y)x^2\}.
 \end{aligned}                                                 \tag{1.2}
\]

The rational identity (1.2) itself holds for every real \(y\), since its
denominator is \((y+1)^2+3>0\); positivity is needed only to interpret the
sum below as a squared elliptic rate.

Let any finite collection, or any countable collection satisfying

\[
 \sum_j w_j\{1+|\kappa(y_j)|\}<\infty,
 \qquad w_j\geq0,
\]

be given, and define

\[
 W=\sum_jw_j,\qquad K=\sum_jw_j\kappa(y_j).         \tag{1.3}
\]

At a fixed source radius, the normalized squared bright rate is therefore

\[
 \boxed{
 {\Lambda^2(x)\over A^2}
 ={1\over2}\{W+K-Wx-Kx^2\}.}                       \tag{1.4}
\]

The linear coefficient is exactly \(-W/2\).  It is independent of every
height \(y_j\), and it is strictly negative for every nonzero collection of
nonnegative shared gate intensities.  In particular,

\[
 {\Lambda^2(x)-\Lambda^2(-x)\over A^2}=-Wx.         \tag{1.5}
\]

Thus no such static mixture is constant on any open angular arc on which
\(x=\sin(2\phi)\) varies.  On a symmetric pair \(x,-x\), its squared-rate
spread is exactly \(A^2W|x|\).  Allowing the heights and nonnegative
intensities to depend on time does not remove this coefficient at any instant
when the gate is on.

This is a static/instantaneous obstruction, not a no-go theorem for
noncommuting time-dependent controls.  A varying gate phase changes the
Hamiltonian axis, and its rotating-frame derivative supplies a diagonal
detuning.  Those operations are analyzed next.

## 3. A bounded finite-sheet preweighting workaround

Before adding any external chirp, there is a simpler exact observation in
the **independent weighted two-level model**.  Suppose the source rates obey

\[
 0<\lambda_-\leq\lambda_i\leq\lambda_+,
 \qquad 0<T< {\pi\over2\lambda_+}.                  \tag{3.1}
\]

Under the common fixed-axis evolution \(e^{-iT\lambda_i\sigma_x}\), a source
coefficient \(a_i\) produces

\[
 z_i(T)=a_i\cos(\lambda_iT),\qquad
 d_i(T)=-ia_i\sin(\lambda_iT).                      \tag{3.2}
\]

For any desired daughter coefficients \(d_i^*\), choose

\[
 a_i={i d_i^*\over\sin(\lambda_iT)}.                \tag{3.3}
\]

Then \(d_i(T)=d_i^*\) exactly, at one common time.  Since sine is positive
and increasing on the interval in (3.1), the preweighting is uniformly
bounded:

\[
 {1\over\sin(\lambda_+T)}
 \leq {1\over\sin(\lambda_iT)}
 \leq {1\over\sin(\lambda_-T)}.                    \tag{3.4}
\]

Thus exact quarter-time synchronization is unnecessary if the \(q^2\)
incoming source coefficients may be prepared with rate-dependent bounded
weights.  This does **not** close the splitter.  It leaves the source remnant

\[
 z_i(T)=i d_i^*\cot(\lambda_iT),                    \tag{3.5}
\]

which is order one, with constants depending only on
\((\lambda_-,\lambda_+,T)\), and C159's Floquet
output has not been shown to realize the required \(q^2\) individual
preweights.  More importantly, on the exact tangential full ladder, Section
2 shows that apparent daughter equalization is only Fourier spreading of a
pointwise phase and creates no physical focus.  Collisions, the C163
source-dependent similarity, and the unforced realization remain open.

## 4. A common chirped pulse on a compact rate interval

Let

\[
 0<\lambda_-\leq\lambda\leq\lambda_+<\infty,
 \qquad
 f(s)=4s(1-s),\qquad g(s)=2s-1,\quad 0\leq s\leq1. \tag{4.1}
\]

With the Pauli matrices \(\sigma_x,\sigma_y,\sigma_z\), consider

\[
 i{d\psi\over dt}=H_\lambda(t/J)\psi,
 \qquad
 H_\lambda(s)=\lambda f(s)\sigma_x+g(s)\sigma_z,
 \qquad 0\leq t\leq J.                             \tag{4.2}
\]

The schedule is common to every \(\lambda\).  At the endpoints,

\[
 H_\lambda(0)=-\sigma_z,\qquad H_\lambda(1)=\sigma_z. \tag{4.3}
\]

Consequently its ground state starts on \(e_1\) and ends on \(e_2\).
The following lemma gives a uniform quantitative transfer.

### Lemma 4.1 (explicit compact-ensemble adiabatic transfer)

Define

\[
 \begin{gathered}
 m=\min\left\{{1\over2},{3\lambda_-\over4}\right\},
 \qquad
 L={2+4\lambda_+^2\over m},\\
 M_1={4\lambda_+\over m^2},
 \qquad
 M_2={8\lambda_+\over m^2}+{8\lambda_+L\over m^3},\\
 C={M_1\over2m}+{M_2\over4m}
   +{M_1L\over4m^2}+{M_1^2\over8m}.
 \end{gathered}                                                \tag{4.4}
\]

If \(\psi(0)=e_1\), then for every
\(\lambda\in[\lambda_-,\lambda_+]\) there is a phase \(\zeta_\lambda\)
such that

\[
 \boxed{
 \|\psi(J)-e^{i\zeta_\lambda}e_2\|_2
 \leq {\sqrt2 C\over J}.}                           \tag{4.5}
\]

The unwanted-state probability is at most \(C^2/J^2\).  Hence with the C161
notation \(J=n^2=b^{-1}\), the state error is at most
\(\sqrt2 Cb=O(n^{-2})\), uniformly over the compact rate interval.

This is population transfer, not coherent phase synchronization.  The lemma
does not assert that \(\zeta_\lambda\) is independent of \(\lambda\).  Indeed
the adiabatic dynamical phase contains

\[
 J\Phi_\lambda(1)=J\int_0^1
       \sqrt{g(s)^2+\lambda^2f(s)^2}\,ds,
 \qquad
 {d\Phi_\lambda(1)\over d\lambda}
 =\int_0^1{\lambda f(s)^2\over
       \sqrt{g(s)^2+\lambda^2f(s)^2}}\,ds>0.        \tag{4.5a}
\]

Thus a separate common-phase or phase-compensation estimate is required
before this lemma can produce physical point focus.  No impossibility is
claimed: special discrete rates or a composite pulse might cancel these
phases.

### Proof

Write

\[
 E=(g^2+\lambda^2f^2)^{1/2}.
\]

Since \(f=1-g^2\), either \(|g|\geq1/2\), in which case \(E\geq1/2\), or
\(|g|\leq1/2\), in which case \(f\geq3/4\) and
\(E\geq3\lambda_-/4\).  Thus

\[
 E\geq m.                                             \tag{4.6}
\]

Choose the continuous angle \(\vartheta\in[0,\pi]\) by

\[
 \cos\vartheta={g\over E},\qquad
 \sin\vartheta={\lambda f\over E}.                  \tag{4.7}
\]

Then

\[
 H_\lambda=E e^{-i\vartheta\sigma_y/2}\sigma_z
                 e^{i\vartheta\sigma_y/2},
 \qquad \vartheta(0)=\pi,\quad\vartheta(1)=0.        \tag{4.8}
\]

The exact differentiation identities are

\[
 \vartheta'
 ={\lambda(gf'-fg')\over E^2}
 =-{2\lambda(1+g^2)\over E^2},                       \tag{4.9}
\]

and

\[
 E'={gg'+\lambda^2ff'\over E}.                       \tag{4.10}
\]

Here \(|g|\leq1\), \(|f|\leq1\), \(|g'|=2\), and \(|f'|\leq4\).
Equations (4.6), (4.9), and (4.10) give

\[
 |E'|\leq L,\qquad |\vartheta'|\leq M_1,\qquad
 |\vartheta''|\leq M_2.                             \tag{4.11}
\]

Pass to the instantaneous eigenbasis and remove the two dynamical phases.
If \(a\) is the excited amplitude and \(c\) the ground amplitude, then,
up to harmless common sign conventions,

\[
 a'={\vartheta'\over2}e^{2iJ\Phi}c,
 \qquad
 c'=-{\vartheta'\over2}e^{-2iJ\Phi}a,
 \qquad
 \Phi'=E,\qquad a(0)=0,\quad c(0)=1.               \tag{4.12}
\]

In particular \(|a|,|c|\leq1\).  Integrating the first equation by parts
once, using \((e^{2iJ\Phi})'=2iJEe^{2iJ\Phi}\), yields

\[
 |a(1)|\leq {1\over J}\left{
 {M_1\over2m}+{M_2\over4m}
 +{M_1L\over4m^2}+{M_1^2\over8m}\right}
 ={C\over J}.                                       \tag{4.13}
\]

Indeed the first term bounds the two endpoints, the next two bound the
\(\vartheta''\) and \(E'\) pieces produced by differentiating
\(\vartheta'/(4E)\), and the last uses
\(|c'|\leq|\vartheta'|/2\).  Since the evolution is unitary, choosing the
phase of the final ground component gives

\[
 \inf_\zeta\|\psi(J)-e^{i\zeta}e_2\|_2
 \leq\sqrt2|a(1)|,
\]

which proves (4.5).  The transition-probability statement follows directly
from \(|a(1)|^2\leq C^2/J^2\).  \(\square\)

## 5. The detuning is exactly a common gate-phase chirp

The diagonal term in (4.2) need not be treated as a separate abstract
control.  Consider the lab-frame transverse pulse

\[
 \widetilde H_\lambda(t)
 =\lambda f(t/J)\{\cos\varphi(t)\sigma_x
                    +\sin\varphi(t)\sigma_y\},       \tag{5.1}
\]

with the common phase

\[
 \varphi(t)=2t-{2t^2\over J}=2J s(1-s),
 \qquad s=t/J.                                      \tag{5.2}
\]

For \(Z=e^{-i\varphi\sigma_z/2}\), the change \(\psi=Z\chi\) gives

\[
 i\dot\chi=\left\{\lambda f(s)\sigma_x
                  -{\dot\varphi\over2}\sigma_z\right\}\chi.
                                                               \tag{5.3}
\]

But \(\dot\varphi=2-4s=-2g(s)\), so (5.3) is exactly (4.2).
Moreover \(\varphi(0)=\varphi(J)=0\); the lab and rotating bases agree at
both endpoints.  Thus Lemma 4.1 is genuinely a common envelope-and-phase
pulse, with no source-dependent setting.  Endpoint agreement removes only
the frame phase; it does not remove the rate-dependent dynamical phase in
(4.5a).

This equivalence is an identity for the weighted C163 two-level blocks.  It
does not prove that a real unforced Navier--Stokes gate can execute the
prescribed chirp while retaining its support, polarization, wake, and
decaying-pump constraints.

## 6. Exact resource mismatch with the current stage ledger

The normalized envelope area of (5.1) is

\[
 \int_0^J f(t/J)\,dt
 =J\int_0^1 4s(1-s)\,ds={2J\over3}.                 \tag{6.1}
\]

Thus the transverse action in the \(\lambda\)-block is
\(2\lambda J/3\), uniformly comparable to \(J\) on the fixed compact rate
interval.

Its phase has total variation \(J\), rising from zero to \(J/2\) and
returning to zero.  The pulse therefore uses bounded \(O(1)\) control over
each of \(J\) normalized time units, not \(J\) microgates of angle
\(b=J^{-1}\).

Merely scaling the physical transverse envelope to \(bf\) while retaining
the phase law (5.2) gives the rotating-frame Hamiltonian

\[
 b\lambda f(s)\sigma_x+g(s)\sigma_z.               \tag{6.2}
\]

Its positive eigenvalue magnitude (half-gap) at \(s=1/2\) is exactly
\(b\lambda\), so it leaves the fixed-gap regime of Lemma 4.1; that lemma
gives no uniform \(O(b)\) transfer bound.
If the phase derivative is also scaled by \(b\), the rotating Hamiltonian is
instead \(bH_\lambda\), and the proof sees effective adiabatic duration

\[
 J_{\rm eff}=bJ=1.                                  \tag{6.3}
\]

The bound (4.5) then gives no vanishing error.  Hence C165 does not fit the
C146/C161 wake powers merely because its step count is \(J=n^2\).  A usable
one-cell theorem still needs one of the following on the same charge-star
geometry:

1. a phase-coherent composite or exactly solvable common pulse with \(O(b)\)
   per-step gate action and \(O(1)\) total action but \(O(b)\) uniform error;
2. a strengthened wake/return estimate that permits \(O(J)\) total action;
3. a source sheet whose physical rates collapse to an \(O(b)\)-narrow
   interval, together with a fixed-area perturbative synchronization lemma;
4. an unforced pump phase law that realizes a robust transfer while also
   preserving the C163 weighted-to-physical energy budget.

No one-cell Navier--Stokes stage and no Millennium conclusion is claimed.
